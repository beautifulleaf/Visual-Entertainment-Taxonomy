## A Feature Film — test: a film of 40 minutes or more made as a single work for cinema, festival, television or streaming release; not anime.
  key: 1. Non-fiction? → A13 Documentary.
  key: 2. Does it belong to a named genre cycle (the narrower leaves of A2–A12: chanbara, kaidan, J-horror, yakuza, noir, pink, Roman Porno, pinky violence, kaiju, tokusatsu, Taiyōzoku)? → skip to that leaf (F1). Otherwise, is it an attested member of a named film movement? → A1 Movements & Schools.
  key: 3. Is erotic display or sexual obsession the film's reason to exist — made for the sex-film circuit, or an art-house film built on it? → A12 Erotic Film. A violent exploitation cycle with erotic elements (pinky violence) is not: it goes on.
  key: 4. Does it aim first to frighten (ghosts, monsters, killers, gore)? → A4 Horror (F3).
  key: 5. Is its premise speculative science — the future, technology, space, giant monsters, transforming heroes? → A5 Science Fiction & Monster (F5). Magic or myth? → A6 Fantasy & Adventure.
  key: 6. Is it set in a past era that defines it, or about war? → A7 Historical, Period & War (F4). A frontier western → A17 Western.
  key: 7. Criminals or police at the centre? → A2 Crime. Suspense, conspiracy or puzzle? → A3 Thriller & Mystery (F6).
  key: 8. Fights, chases and stunts carry it? → A8 Action & Martial Arts.
  key: 9. Songs carry the story? → A11 Musical & Music. Laughter first? → A10 Comedy. A love story? → A9 Romance. Otherwise → A14 Drama (F7).
### A1 Movements & Schools — Axis: the named movement (membership attested in its reference sources).
A1-1 Japanese New Wave: the Nūberu bāgu: films of Ōshima, Imamura, Yoshida, Shinoda, Teshigahara, Hani and their peers (c. 1959–1975) that broke with studio humanism through politics, sexuality and formal rupture (Cruel Story of Youth, Woman in the Dunes, Death by Hanging). Members with a genre-cycle home (Harakiri, Kwaidan, Branded to Kill) sit in that cycle and carry the movement as a tag (D1). [aka Nūberu bāgu; Shōchiku Nouvelle Vague]
### A2 Crime — Axis: whose side the story takes and its milieu.
A2-1 Yakuza Film: yakuza eiga: films of the Japanese gangster world, from the chivalrous ninkyō films of 1960s Toei to the documentary-style jitsuroku films (Battles Without Honor and Humanity) and the V-cinema and Kitano cycles (Sonatine). [aka yakuza eiga; ninkyō eiga; jitsuroku eiga]
A2-2 Gangster Film: films centred on organised criminals and their rise and fall outside the named national cycles. [aka mob film]
A2-3 Police & Detective Film: films centred on police or detectives working a case (Stray Dog, High and Low). [aka police procedural; detective film]
A2-4 Crime Drama: films about crime, its perpetrators and its effects, told as drama rather than suspense or gang epic (Vengeance Is Mine, The Third Murder). [aka crime film]
A2-5 Film Noir & Neo-Noir: crime films in the noir manner — fatalism, low-key style, corrupt cities — including Japanese noir (Pale Flower, Branded to Kill). [aka noir; neo-noir]
A2-6 Pinky Violence: Toei's 1970s cycle of women's revenge, delinquent-girl (sukeban) and women-in-prison films, with violence and eroticism (Female Prisoner #701: Scorpion). [aka sukeban films; women-in-prison film]
### A3 Thriller & Mystery — Axis: the source of suspense.
A3-1 Psychological Thriller: suspense built on disturbed minds, obsession and manipulation (Cure, Confessions). [aka ]
A3-2 Mystery Film: whodunits and puzzle films built on detection, including honkaku adaptations (The Inugamis). [aka whodunit; detective mystery]
A3-3 Survival & Death-Game Thriller: films in which characters are forced into a lethal contest or ordeal (Battle Royale, As the Gods Will). [aka death game; survival thriller]
### A4 Horror — Axis: the source of fear.
A4-1 J-Horror: the Japanese horror cycle of the late 1990s–2000s: vengeful onryō, curses carried by media and technology, slow dread (Ring, Ju-On, Pulse, Dark Water). [aka Japanese horror; J-horror cycle]
A4-2 Kaidan Film: kaidan eiga: ghost-story films drawn from Edo and folk tales (Ugetsu, Kwaidan, Onibaba, Kuroneko). [aka kaidan eiga; Japanese ghost story film]
A4-3 Supernatural Horror: horror from ghosts, demons and curses outside the named Japanese cycles (House, Noroi). [aka ]
A4-4 Body Horror: horror of the body transformed, invaded or fused with machines (Tetsuo: The Iron Man). [aka biological horror]
A4-5 Splatter & Gore Film: horror built on graphic violence, mutilation and torture (Audition, Ichi the Killer, Tokyo Gore Police). [aka splatter film; ero guro; extreme cinema]
A4-6 Zombie Film: horror of the walking dead (One Cut of the Dead, Versus). [aka ]
A4-7 Found-Footage Horror: horror presented as recovered amateur or documentary footage (Noroi: The Curse). [aka found footage]
A4-8 Slasher Film: horror built on a killer stalking and murdering victims one by one (Evil Dead Trap). [aka slasher]
A4-9 Psychological Horror: horror whose dread comes from disturbed minds, unreality and inescapable spaces rather than monsters or gore (Strange Circus, Haze, Exit 8). [aka ]
### A5 Science Fiction & Monster — Axis: the speculative premise.
A5-1 Kaiju Film: kaiju eiga: films built on giant monsters — Godzilla, Mothra, Rodan, Gamera — and their destruction of cities (Godzilla 1954, Shin Godzilla). [aka kaiju eiga; giant monster film]
A5-2 Tokusatsu Hero Film: live-action special-effects films of costumed transforming heroes (Ultraman, Kamen Rider, Super Sentai). [aka tokusatsu; henshin hero]
A5-3 Science Fiction Film: speculative films of the future, technology, space and alien contact outside the narrower leaves (Virus, Summer Time Machine Blues). [aka sci-fi]
A5-4 Cyberpunk: high-tech, low-life science fiction of cybernetic bodies and urban decay, with its Japanese punk-industrial strand (Tetsuo, Burst City). [aka Japanese cyberpunk]
A5-5 Disaster & Post-Apocalyptic Film: films of catastrophe, collapse and survival after it (Japan Sinks, Virus). [aka disaster film; post-apocalyptic film]
### A6 Fantasy & Adventure — Axis: the kind of wonder.
A6-1 Fantasy Film: live-action films of magic, myth and invented worlds (The Great Yokai War). [aka ]
A6-2 Adventure Film: journeys and quests in exotic or perilous settings. [aka ]
A6-3 Superhero & Manga Action Adaptation: live-action adaptations of superhero and battle manga whose premise is extraordinary powers (Gantz, Death Note). [aka superhero film; live-action manga adaptation]
### A7 Historical, Period & War — Axis: the era and what the past is used for.
A7-1 Jidaigeki: period drama set before the Meiji Restoration in which the past is the subject — manners, politics, family and fate rather than swordplay (Sansho the Bailiff, The Life of Oharu, Twilight Samurai). [aka period drama (Japanese); historical drama]
A7-2 Chanbara: samurai swordplay films in which sword combat carries the drama (Seven Samurai, Yojimbo, Zatoichi, 13 Assassins). [aka samurai film; swordplay film; chambara]
A7-3 War Film: films about war and soldiers' experience of it (Fires on the Plain, The Burmese Harp, The Human Condition). [aka anti-war film]
A7-4 Historical Drama: films whose past era defines them outside jidaigeki and war: Meiji, Taishō and Shōwa stories, real events and biopics (The Story of the Last Chrysanthemum, Sanshiro Sugata excepted as martial arts). [aka biopic; historical film]
A7-5 Atomic Bomb Film: genbaku eiga: films about the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the hibakusha (Children of Hiroshima, Black Rain). [aka genbaku eiga; hibakusha cinema]
### A8 Action & Martial Arts — Axis: the fighting tradition.
A8-1 Martial Arts Film: films built on unarmed or weapons combat in modern or timeless settings (The Street Fighter), outside chanbara. [aka karate film]
A8-2 Action Film: films carried by fights, chases, gunplay and stunts outside the narrower leaves (Shinobi, Alive). [aka action thriller]
### A9 Romance — Axis: tone.
A9-1 Romantic Drama: love stories told as drama (Love Letter, Norwegian Wood). [aka melodrama of love]
A9-2 Romantic Comedy: love stories told for laughter. [aka romcom]
### A10 Comedy — Axis: comic mode.
A10-1 Comedy Film: films aimed first at laughter (Tampopo, Swing Girls). [aka ]
A10-2 Comedy-Drama: films that balance comedy with serious drama (Tokyo Sonata). [aka dramedy]
A10-3 Black Comedy & Satire: comedy of death, taboo and social satire (The Funeral, The Taste of Tea). [aka dark comedy; satire]
### A11 Musical & Music — Axis: how music carries the film.
A11-1 Musical & Music Film: films carried by song and music, including band and idol films (Linda Linda Linda, The Happiness of the Katakuris). [aka musical film; music film]
### A12 Erotic Film — Axis: the circuit it was made for.
A12-1 Pink Film: pinku eiga: independent Japanese softcore features made from the 1960s for the pink-film circuit, a training ground for directors (Daydream). [aka pinku eiga]
A12-2 Roman Porno: Nikkatsu's studio line of erotic features (1971–1988) under which Kumashiro, Tanaka and Sone made auteur films (Flower and Snake, A Woman Called Sada Abe). [aka Nikkatsu Roman Porno; romanporno]
A12-3 Erotic Drama: art-house and mainstream films whose subject is sexual obsession (In the Realm of the Senses, The Housemaid). [aka erotic film]
### A13 Documentary — Axis: mode and subject.
A13-1 Documentary Film: non-fiction films (The Emperor's Naked Army Marches On, Minamata series). [aka ]
### A14 Drama — Axis: the drama's subject (family, youth, society, the individual).
A14-1 Shomingeki: shomin-geki: dramas of ordinary lower-middle-class urban life, centred on the family and the workplace, the home genre of Ozu, Naruse and Kinoshita (Tokyo Story, Floating Clouds). [aka shomin-geki; home drama]
A14-2 Family Drama: dramas of families, parents and children outside the classic shomingeki (Shoplifters, Nobody Knows, Like Father, Like Son). [aka family film (drama)]
A14-3 Coming-of-Age Film: seishun eiga: youth and school films about growing up (All About Lily Chou-Chou, Kids Return). [aka seishun eiga; youth film; teen film]
A14-4 Social Drama: dramas of social conditions, poverty, work, institutions and injustice (Ikiru, The Ballad of Narayama, Departures). [aka social problem film; social realism]
A14-5 Character Study: dramas centred on one person's inner life, grief, solitude or change (Drive My Car, Perfect Days). [aka psychological drama; art-house drama]
A14-6 Melodrama: emotionally heightened dramas of suffering and sacrifice, especially women's films (haha-mono mother films, The Life of Oharu). [aka haha-mono; women's film]
A14-7 Sports Film: dramas built on a sport and its competition. [aka ]
A14-8 Animal Film: live-action films centred on an animal and its bond with people (The Story of Hachikō, The Adventures of Milo and Otis). [aka dog film; animal story]
A14-9 Taiyōzoku Film: the 'Sun Tribe' youth films of 1956 adapted from Ishihara Shintarō — affluent, amoral postwar youth (Season of the Sun, Crazed Fruit). [aka Sun Tribe films; taiyōzoku eiga]

## G Unscripted Formats — test: a cross-border unscripted format (≥3 versions in ≥2 countries); its national versions are kinds.

PRECEDENCE
F1 Named cycle before movement: A film with a home in a named genre cycle (chanbara, kaidan, J-horror, yakuza, film noir, pink film, Roman Porno, pinky violence, kaiju, tokusatsu, Taiyōzoku) goes to that cycle, even when a movement's reference sources list it as a member. A movement leaf keeps the attested members with no cycle home; every attested member carries the movement as a tag (D1).
F2 Named cycle before general genre: A named cycle with its own canon (J-horror, kaiju eiga, yakuza eiga, pink film) beats the general genre leaf (supernatural horror, monster film, gangster film, sexploitation).
F3 Ghosts before period: A film whose story turns on ghosts or the supernatural is horror (kaidan eiga) even when set in the Edo period; period setting alone does not make jidaigeki win.
F4 Swords before period: A jidaigeki whose drama is carried by sword combat is chanbara; a period film of manners, politics or family is jidaigeki.
F5 Giant monsters: A film built on a giant monster (kaiju) is Kaiju Film whether or not a costumed hero appears; a costumed transforming hero with no giant monster at its centre is Tokusatsu Hero Film.
F6 Crime vs thriller: Criminals or police and their world at the centre → Crime; suspense built on a threat to an ordinary protagonist, a conspiracy or a puzzle → Thriller & Mystery.
F7 Drama vs romance vs comedy: A love story as the main line → Romance; aimed at laughter first → Comedy; otherwise Drama, divided by its subject.
F9 Framing devices: A fantastical or science-fiction device that only frames a love story or a youth drama (a time slip, a letter from the future) does not make the film fantasy or science fiction: it is placed by the story it frames (A9, A14-3).
F10 Leaf examples follow the key: When a film named as a leaf example would be sent elsewhere by the key, the key wins and the example is replaced; examples never override the key.
F8 Anime: Japanese animation is always E Anime, never an animated-feature leaf, whatever list it was selected from.

MODIFIERS (tags/filters, never nodes)
Country of origin — a filter (Wikidata P495 first, as in the selection rule; a co-production counts in each of its countries, D8). National cinemas are never divisions; national cycles and movements defined by what the films are (J-horror, Japanese New Wave) are leaves.
Decade and period of release — a filter.
Director, studio, writer — tags; studio-branded cycles are leaves only where the field names them as a genre (Nikkatsu Roman Porno).
Setting — jidaigeki (pre-1868) or gendaigeki (contemporary) as a filter on every Japanese film; a leaf (Jidaigeki) exists only for the period drama genre.
Release format — theatrical, V-cinema (direct-to-video), TV movie, streaming: a filter.
Movement — a tag on every attested member of a named movement (Japanese New Wave), including members placed in a genre cycle (D1).
Format — for anime: TV series, film, OVA, ONA, special (D4). For series: miniseries, limited, ongoing.
Asian drama — the drama database's scope (Korea, China, Japan, Taiwan, Thailand, the Philippines, Hong Kong, Singapore) as a filter on Television Series (D3).
Source material (manga, novel, true story) — a tag.
Language, rating, runtime, platform — tags.
Audience counts (Letterboxd, IMDb, MAL, MDL) — kept internal (licensing); never shown.
