Restructure R3 (November 2026)

Your review asked for: a populated Design family; fuller, organised Visual Art leaves; Film & Television regrouped into Film, Television and Animation with the retired Asian Drama family gone; and leaves at the granularity of the Culinary Taxonomy instead of catch-alls such as a 630-title "Comedy Film". Later requests during the work are included: web series out, live and sports television in, retired leaves removed, a Copy button, era/country/director as fundamentals, complete rule-based catalogues instead of hand-picked works, "View all" archives, the erotic-art line, every subgenre represented, and TSPDT.

Film & Television

  • Families: A Film (A1–A20 by movement and genre, A21 Short Film), B Television (B1–B11 series, B12 Serial Drama Traditions, B13–B15 unscripted formats, B16 Live Event & Sports Television), C Animation (C1–C11 anime, C12–C14 non-Japanese features, series and shorts). Old codes map to new ones in taxonomy/r3/ft_migration.csv.
  • 899 leaves, 38,109 titles (332 leaves before); median leaf 28, largest 197; every leaf over 40 titles is divided into sections along a stated axis. No catch-all leaf remains: e.g. Comedy is now 58 leaves by comic engine (Zoolander is in Parody & Spoof), Drama 38, Horror 38.
  • Fundamentals (era, country, director): named era- and country-bounded cycles are leaves (Pre-Code Hollywood, Brat Pack, commedia sexy all'italiana, Ozploitation, Category III, Heimatfilm, Telefoni Bianchi ...); genres the field divides by era, nation or auteur are sectioned that way (giallo by Bava/Argento/Fulci/Martino; kaiju Shōwa/Heisei/Millennium/Reiwa; noir by phase and national noir; sitcoms by US and UK eras). The page has country, decade and director filters.
  • Coverage: every sub-family was audited against IMDb's genre interests, AllMovie, Wikipedia genre lists and the TSPDT lists; 36 missing subgenres were added and 1,563 titles filled empty or thin leaves (Pink Film: 0 → 63, incl. the Roman Porno Reboot and Antiporno).
  • B16 Live Event & Sports Television (new, 28 leaves): sports by sport and event, multi-sport events, professional wrestling, award ceremonies, song contests, pageants, parades and state occasions, telethons, concerts, theatre/opera/ballet broadcasts, stand-up specials. News is out of scope.
  • Out of scope: web series and other internet-native series (to the future Interactive Entertainment Taxonomy; 112 titles, finder rows kept); subscription streaming series are television. Retired leaves are no longer shown.

Visual Art

  • N Design (new, 34 leaves): furniture and lighting, product and industrial, and graphic design schools, ~355 designed objects (rule: held by MoMA or the leaf's national collection) and their designers.
  • 1,672 leaves, 71,669 kinds (36,705 before). Every leaf has sections along a stated axis and "View all" links to complete external catalogues and archives (Archnet, Islamic Architectural Heritage, Vogue Runway, Beazley Archive, Shahnama Project, Corpus Vitrearum, national heritage registers, museum collection searches).
  • Complete catalogues by rule, not picks: tradition-led leaves list every gathered building, site, manuscript or titled object that passes the rule stated in the leaf's axis (e.g. ~12,000 buildings in Architecture; 741 named manuscripts in book arts), sectioned by school, phase, dynasty or building type, plus a computed Collections group (holding institutions ranked by object count).
  • Catch-all artist buckets broken up: ~3,200 artists moved to their movement or school; 73 new movement and school leaves (Vienna Secession, Peredvizhniki, Gutai, Young British Artists, Magnum Photos ...). Modern Art and Contemporary Art remain as residual leaves, grouped by named local groups and then by country.
  • One home per maker: 717 makers placed in more than one leaf keep a primary home; other leaves keep a cross-reference row.
  • New H7 Haute Couture & Fashion Design (174 designers; View all: Vogue Runway, the Costume Institute, Kyoto Costume Institute).

Rulings this release

  • S3, erotic art or pornography: two questions about the work as made — is it explicit, and was it made for the art/mainstream circuit or the adult trade? Explicit + adult trade → Pleasure Taxonomies; everything else here (non-explicit adult-trade products only when the field names them as a genre with a canon). The circuit at making decides, not later fame.
  • Selection: every film on TSPDT's Starting List and 21st Century Starting List is selected (~21,000 films not yet placed; placement is the next step).
