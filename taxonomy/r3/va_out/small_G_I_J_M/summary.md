# small_G_I_J_M — Metalwork/Arms/Jewellery, Woodwork/Lacquer/Furniture, Photography, New Media (219 leaves)

## What changed
- Every leaf is sectioned with the standard vocabulary (Sub-types · Schools & phases · Centres & workshops · works sections · Collections · Makers) and has an `axis`. Artist-led leaves (I3-6…I3-18 decorative styles, all J1 and M leaves) group makers by named circle/phase (Provoke, New York School, Magnum, Life, Soviet and Vietnam photojournalists, the Black Trinity, Becher classes, Maciunas/Japanese/German Fluxus, Zaj, Celant's Arte Povera group, net.art vs post-internet, computer-art pioneers), falling back to country of activity.
- Glosses cleaned: machine notes (route resolved, excluded rows, Met counts, now T…, Q-ids) removed; artist glosses rebuilt from evidence (role, birth year, country); about 110 decorative-arts makers got specific roles. Possession back-links kept as "· Possession: X (B3)", with the URL in `source`.
- 781 attested kinds added from knowledge (sub-types, centres, workshops, makers, named masterpieces with holding museum), each with a Wikipedia (a few Pipedia) URL.
- Works catalogues (1,888 rows over 98 leaves), one rule per leaf, stated in the axis. Most object leaves: "every gathered object with a descriptive museum title (≥ 3 words, bare object names excluded), one per title, the 40 earliest-dated where more qualify", sectioned by object type, or by the field's own axis (Akan goldweights geometric vs figurative; krisses by region; Fijian clubs by type; Japanese swords by blade type). Japanese swords, armour, maki-e and lacquer use "every gathered object designated a National Treasure", sectioned by period. Land art uses every gathered land-art site with sitelinks ≥ 2.
- Collections (428 rows over 102 leaves): computed from the gathered object files (islamic, east_asia, smithsonian, world_extra, world_folk, europe_folk, gap_sweep, americas/africa/SSEA/himalaya). Every institution with ≥ 10 objects is listed, or the top 8.
- 483 `catalogues` links, at least one per leaf: Met / V&A / British Museum / Smithsonian / Cooper Hewitt / MoMA / Tate searches, Wallace Collection, Royal Armouries, ColBase, bunka.nii.ac.jp, e-Museum Korea, NPM Taipei, Goskatalog, Wereldmuseum, Museo del Oro, Epact, HSM Oxford, MIMO, MIMEd, Horniman, Tarisio Cozio, Rhizome ArtBase and Net Art Anthology, EAI, Video Data Bank, LIMA, ADA, Dia, Magnum, World Press Photo, UNESCO ICH and others.

## Doubtful items to check
- Data-driven works in Russian (Goskatalog) and Japanese (kunishitei) keep their original titles. Some museum-record sections are coarse ("Other objects").
- Matching in the object files is by culture/dynasty label plus material, so there is some noise. Examples: ceramics excluded from metal leaves by a filter; the Bedouin node includes Rasulid metal; G1-45 English silver comes only from Cooper Hewitt.
- Group placements that need care: Edith Heath (Bauhaus, under "Associated"); Irina Zatulovskaya and Ger van Elk (Arte Povera, under "International associates"); Maria Eichhorn (Fluxus, under "Later associates"); Chris Killip (Humanistic Photography); the decorative-style leaves I3-6…I3-13 keep the heterogeneous maker lists from the input (e.g. Levi Strauss, Betsy Ross), grouped by country.
- Wikipedia URLs for some added kinds point to the parent article where no dedicated article was certain (e.g. Bidri techniques → Bidriware, Tenka-Goken swords → Tenka-Goken).
- Pierre Gole appears twice (I1-23 Boulle Work and I3-8 Louis XIV); the validator warns about this, which is harmless.
