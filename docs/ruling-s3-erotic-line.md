## The test: two questions, asked of the work as made
**1. Is it explicit?** Explicit = it shows unsimulated sexual activity or genitals in sexual use as its content (hardcore). Nudity, simulated sex, erotic subject matter, softcore and suggestion are *not* explicit.

**2. Which circuit was it made for and first released through?**
- *Art and mainstream circuit*: artists' studios, workshops and patrons; galleries, museums and art publishing; mainstream print trades (e.g. the ukiyo-e publishers); cinemas, festivals and broadcast outside the adult trade; mainstream publishers of comics and books.
- *Adult trade*: production and distribution that exists to sell sexual arousal: adult-video and adult-website studios, adult theatres showing hardcore, adult magazines and photo trades, adult imprints and labels (hentai OVA labels, adult doujin circles, eroge publishers), cam and creator platforms.

## Precedence sentences
- **Circuit at making decides, not later fame.** A hardcore feature made for the adult trade stays EP even if critics and archives later canonise it; VE keeps a finder row. A work made in the art or mainstream circuit stays VE even when it is explicit.
- **Explicitness alone never decides.** Explicit art in museums is VE; non-explicit products of the adult trade without a canon are EP.
- **Later reissue does not change the circuit**: a museum acquiring an adult-trade object, or an adult label reissuing an art film, does not move it.
- **Mixed circuits**: when a work was made for both (a commercial print series sold through the regular publishers, as shunga was), the art/mainstream circuit wins.
- **Unclear cases** (e.g. Tom of Finland: drawn for physique magazines, now in museum collections) go by the circuit of first publication, here the adult trade → EP, with a VE finder row; the owner may overrule case by case.
