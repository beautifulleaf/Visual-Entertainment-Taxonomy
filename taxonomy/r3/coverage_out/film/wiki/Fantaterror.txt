'''Fantaterror''' is a term for Spanish fantasy and horror cinema, particularly films produced in the 1960s and 1970s.<ref name="Sin_nombre-p699-1">{{cite book |access-date=26 April 2020 |last=García |date=4 September 2017 |first=Juan Antonio Gómez |language=es |page=373 |publisher=Midac, SL |title=Los Derechos Humanos en el cine español. |isbn=978-84-9148-362-5 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=b-hGDwAAQBAJ&dq=cine+espa%C3%B1ol+fantaterror&pg=PA373}}</ref> The term combines the Spanish words ''fantástico'' ("fantastic") and ''terror'' ("horror").<ref>{{cite book |last=Aldana Reyes |first=Xavier |chapter=Fantaterror: Gothic Monsters in the Golden Age of Spanish B-Movie Horror, 1968–80 |title=B-Movie Gothic: International Perspectives |editor-last=Edwards |editor-first=Justin D. |editor2-last=Höglund |editor2-first=Johan |publisher=Edinburgh University Press |year=2018 |pages=95–107 |isbn=9781474423441 |url=https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/bmovie-gothic/fantaterror-gothic-monsters-in-the-golden-age-of-spanish-bmovie-horror-196880/A93C69B2BC2BD2F0F69884E972211226}}</ref> The term has also been used more broadly to refer to Spanish supernatural cinema.<ref>{{cite web |title=Spanish Horror Cinema: Industry, Political Trauma and the Gothic Imaginary |url=https://miskatonicinstitute.com/events/spanish-horror-cinema-industry-political-trauma-and-the-gothic-imaginary-london-online/ |website=Miskatonic Institute of Horror Studies |date=8 October 2020}}</ref>

Considered a genuine Spanish cinematographic tradition and identity<ref name="Sin_nombre-p699-2">{{cite web |date=25 April 2011 |access-date=26 April 2020 |language=es |title=Días de cine - 'El cine fantástico español' |website=RTVE |url=https://www.rtve.es/alacarta/videos/dias-de-cine/dias-cine-cine-fantastico-espanol/1082513/}}</ref> it did not obtain, in general, a positive reception from contemporary critics.<ref name="Sin_nombre-p699-3">{{cite book |access-date=26 April 2020 |last1=Bastías |last2=Barahona |date=1995 |first1=Luis Pérez |first2=Fernando Alonso |language=es |pages=162–163 |publisher=F Imideal |title=Las mentiras sobre el cine español |isbn=978-84-8135-068-5 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ZMJZAAAAMAAJ&q=cine+espa%C3%B1ol+fantaterror}}<!-- auto-translated from Spanish by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> However, several of its directors such as [[Jesús Franco]], Narciso Ibáñez Serrador, [[Paul Naschy]], Antonio Gracia José (Pierrot), León Klimovsky and [[Amando de Ossorio]] achieved national and international commercial success<ref>{{cite web |access-date=13 May 2020 |author=Maribel Baena |date=30 November 2017 |language=es |title=Historia del cine de terror en España |url=https://www.zonared.com/reportajes/historia-cine-terror-espana/3/ |website=www.zonared.com}}<!-- auto-translated from Spanish by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> and some of the titles are considered cult films.<ref name="Sin_nombre-p699-4">{{cite web |access-date=26 April 2020 |author=Carles Rull |date=27 February 2015 |language=es-ES |title=¿Qué películas de terror español son de culto en Estados Unidos? |url=https://blogs.20minutos.es/cine-el-cielo-sobre-tatooine/2015/02/27/que-peliculas-de-terror-espanol-son-de-culto-en-estados-unidos/ |website=20minutos.es}}<!-- auto-translated from Spanish by Module:CS1 translator --></ref>

Film scholar Antonio Lázaro-Reboll examines the Spanish horror boom of 1968–1975 in his 2012 study ''[[Spanish Horror Film]]''. His analysis challenges interpretations of the period's films as merely imitative of foreign horror cinema, emphasizing the diversity of their production contexts and the importance of contemporary critical reception.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Sánchez Trigos |first=Rubén |title=Antonio Lázaro-Reboll, Spanish horror film, Edinburgh, University Press Edinburg, 2012 |journal=Brumal: Revista de Investigación sobre lo Fantástico |year=2013 |volume=1 |issue=2 |pages=403–406 |doi=10.5565/rev/brumal.57 |language=es}}</ref>
