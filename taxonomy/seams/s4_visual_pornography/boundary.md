# S4: the visual-pornography boundary (VE ↔ EP), as decided 2026-11-07
**Model A (exclusive):** each work has one home, set by ruling S3 (`docs/ruling-s3-erotic-line.md`): explicitness × the circuit the work was made for. The side without the work keeps one Finder row. **EP names makers, not titles** (amended B16, `b16_amendment.md`). A title appears in EP only as an example inside a director's, studio's or platform's gloss. So a VE title list has no EP counterpart. EP's equivalent is the maker.

## Test sequence (ask in order, about the work as made)
1. **Minors or no consent?** If anyone depicted is under 18, or the work was made or shared without everyone's consent, it is excluded from both taxonomies (EP B1, B2). Stop.
2. **Explicit?** Unsimulated sex, or genitals in sexual use, shown as the content. If not explicit, apply S1 rule 2 or 3: a genre with a canon goes to VE, and a trade segment without one goes to EP.
3. **Circuit at making and first release?** Art or mainstream (studio or patron, gallery, festival, mainstream cinema or broadcast, mainstream publisher) goes to **VE** by genre. Adult trade (adult cinema or video, adult magazine, adult label, adult site, cam or fan platform) goes to **EP**, filed under its maker.
4. **Both?** If the work was made outside the trade and shown through both circuits, the art circuit wins and it goes to VE. Experimental form alone does not move a commercial adult release.
5. **Later fame never moves a work.** Museum acquisition, restoration and canon lists do not count, and neither does reissue by an adult label.
6. **In EP: file the maker.** File it under the maker who made it for the trade (director, studio, publisher, label, platform), in the leaf for the practice (II-K-1, 4, 5, 8–15). If no maker meets the bar, file it at section level and add a Finder row.

## The 36 TSPDT adult titles (`../ep_handover_tspdt_adult.csv`)
**The seven gay landmarks, decided film by film:**
- *The Fire Island Kids* (De Rome, 1971): **VE FT-A20-2 Underground Film**. A private 8 mm short made among friends, screened privately and at underground shows, and never made for the trade.
- *The Erotic Films of Peter De Rome* (1973): **VE FT-A20-2**. A compilation of those shorts, distributed by Grove Press, an avant-garde publisher and distributor. That is the art circuit; confirm the distributor when fetching sources.
- *L.A. Plays Itself* (Halsted, 1972): **VE FT-A20-2** (mixed circuit, art wins). Halsted shot it over about three years as a self-financed personal film outside any adult producer. Gay adult houses showed it, and so did underground venues. MoMA's later acquisition is not the reason.
- *Sex Garage* (Halsted, 1972): **EP II-K-1.3, Fred Halsted (adult-trade works)**. A short made quickly as a hardcore programme for the gay adult cinemas, so the circuit at making is the trade. This is the closest call; the owner may overrule.
- *Boys in the Sand* (Poole, 1971): **EP II-K-1.3, Wakefield Poole**. A commercial production with a paid-admission run at a New York adult house, made for that audience. Its later fame as a crossover hit does not move it.
- *Bijou* (Poole, 1972): **EP, Wakefield Poole**. It went through the same commercial adult release, and its experimental form does not change the circuit (step 4).
- *Falconhead Part II: The Maneaters* (Michael Zen, 1984): **EP II-K-1.4, Falcon Studios**. A studio video product of the gay adult trade.
