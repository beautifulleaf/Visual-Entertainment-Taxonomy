TAB KEY
1. Is it Japanese animation (anime), in any format — TV series, film, OVA, ONA, special? → E Anime; format is a filter (D4). Animation from elsewhere is placed by its form below (a feature → A15 Animated Feature).
2. Is it an unscripted format licensed across countries (talent competition, dating, cooking, game show, docusoap)? → G Unscripted Formats.
3. Is it a long-running serial in a named serial tradition — telenovela, dizi, Indian daily soap, soap opera? → D Serial Drama Traditions.
4. Any other scripted series or miniseries, from anywhere — including the East and Southeast Asian drama traditions (K-drama, Chinese costume drama, asadora, taiga drama, lakorn)? → C Television Series (D3: Asian drama is a filter, not a family).
5. A film of 40 minutes or less? → B Short Film. Otherwise → A Feature Film (the Academy's 40-minute line).

## C Television Series — test: a series made for television or streaming — scripted series and miniseries, and documentary series — anywhere, outside the named long-running serial traditions, anime and unscripted entertainment.
  key: 0. Non-fiction (a documentary series)? → C8 Documentary Series (TV7).
  key: 1. Animated, and not anime? → C6 Animated Series (TV1).
  key: 2. Made first for children or tweens (live action)? → C7 Children's Series.
  key: 3. An East or Southeast Asian drama in a named tradition — K-drama romance, sageuk, makjang, Chinese costume drama, wuxia/xianxia drama, idol drama, asadora, taiga drama, Japanese trendy drama, lakorn, BL drama? → C2 (D3). Asian dramas outside those traditions (thrillers, medical, fantasy) go by genre below with 'Asian drama' as a filter.
  key: 4. Science fiction, fantasy, horror, the supernatural or superheroes? → C5 Genre Series.
  key: 5. Criminals, police, detectives, spies or a thriller plot? → C3 Crime & Thriller Series.
  key: 6. Laughter first? → C4 Comedy Series. Otherwise → C1 Drama Series, by subject.
### C1 Drama Series — Axis: subject (family, medicine, law, politics, the past, youth).
C1-1 Family Drama Series: series about families and their generations (Six Feet Under, This Is Us, Succession, Reply 1988). [aka family saga]
C1-2 Medical Drama: series set in hospitals and medical practice (ER, Grey's Anatomy, House, Hospital Playlist). [aka hospital drama]
C1-3 Legal Drama Series: series about lawyers and the law's own world (L.A. Law, The Good Wife, Extraordinary Attorney Woo) (TV4). [aka courtroom series]
C1-4 Political Drama Series: series of government, elections and power (The West Wing, House of Cards, Borgen). [aka ]
C1-5 Teen Drama: series about adolescents and high school (Beverly Hills, 90210, Skins, Euphoria, Sex Education excepted as comedy). [aka teen series]
C1-6 Period Drama Series: series set in a defining past, costume and historical drama (Downton Abbey, The Crown, Mad Men, Rome). [aka costume drama; historical drama series]
C1-7 War Drama Series: series about war and soldiers (Band of Brothers, The Pacific, Generation Kill). [aka military drama]
C1-8 Western Series: series of the American frontier (Gunsmoke, Bonanza, Deadwood, Yellowstone as neo-western). [aka TV western]
C1-9 Docudrama Series: dramatisations of real events and lives (Chernobyl, When They See Us, The People v. O. J. Simpson). [aka biographical series; true-story drama]
C1-10 Workplace & Professional Drama: series built on a profession or workplace outside medicine and law (The Newsroom, Mad Men excepted as period, The Bear, Misaeng). [aka workplace drama]
C1-11 Sports Drama Series: series built on a sport and its teams (Friday Night Lights, Ted Lasso excepted as comedy). [aka ]
C1-12 Drama Series: dramas of character and society outside the narrower leaves (The Leftovers, Normal People, The Crown excepted as period). [aka serial drama]
C1-13 Romantic Drama Series: series whose main line is a love story, outside the Asian drama traditions (Outlander excepted as fantasy, Bridgerton, Normal People). [aka romance series]
C1-14 Erotic Drama Series: series whose subject is sexual desire and display, including the Indian erotic web series of the streaming platforms (Gandii Baat, Mastram; Spartacus excepted as period). [aka erotic web series]
### C2 East & Southeast Asian Drama Traditions — Axis: the named tradition (D3: 'Asian drama' is a filter; Asian dramas outside these traditions are placed by genre).
C2-1 K-Drama: the Korean miniseries tradition of romance and melodrama — trendy, fated and rom-com love stories in 16–20 episodes (Winter Sonata, Guardian: The Lonely and Great God, Crash Landing on You, Coffee Prince). [aka Korean drama; Korean miniseries; K-drama romance]
C2-2 Sageuk: Korean historical drama of the Joseon, Goryeo and Three Kingdoms courts (Dae Jang Geum, Moon Embracing the Sun, Mr. Sunshine, Kingdom). [aka Korean historical drama; fusion sageuk]
C2-3 Makjang: Korean melodrama of outrageous twists — birth secrets, revenge, chaebol families, affairs — often in daily and weekend slots (The Penthouse, Temptation of Wife, The World of the Married). [aka makjang drama; Korean family melodrama]
C2-4 Chinese Costume Drama: guzhuang: Chinese historical and palace drama of the imperial past (Story of Yanxi Palace, Nirvana in Fire, Empresses in the Palace, Meteor Garden excepted as idol drama). [aka guzhuang; palace drama; gongdou]
C2-5 Wuxia & Xianxia Drama: Chinese drama of martial heroes (wuxia) and of immortal cultivation and celestial romance (xianxia) (The Legend of the Condor Heroes, The Untamed, Eternal Love, Love Between Fairy and Devil). [aka wuxia drama; xianxia drama; xuanhuan]
C2-6 Idol Drama: Taiwanese and Chinese youth romance with pop-idol casts, from Meteor Garden (2001) to the modern Chinese campus and urban romances (It Started with a Kiss, Go Go Squid!, A Love So Beautiful). [aka ouxiang ju; Taiwanese drama; C-drama romance]
C2-7 Asadora: NHK's morning serial (renzoku terebi shōsetsu) of a woman's life across decades (Oshin, Amachan, Carnation). [aka renzoku terebi shōsetsu]
C2-8 Taiga Drama: NHK's year-long historical drama of a figure of Japanese history (Sanada Maru, Ryōmaden, The 13 Lords of the Shogun). [aka taiga dorama]
C2-9 Japanese Trendy Drama: the Japanese prime-time drama (dorama) of urban romance and work in one-cour seasons, from the trendy dramas of Fuji TV's Monday 9 pm slot (Tokyo Love Story, Long Vacation, Nodame Cantabile, We Married as a Job!). [aka dorama; getsuku; J-drama]
C2-10 Lakorn: Thai television drama (lakhon), with its romance, revenge and slap-kiss conventions (Love Destiny, The Crown Princess, Girl from Nowhere excepted as thriller). [aka lakhon; Thai drama]
C2-11 Boys' Love Drama: BL series — romance between young men — from Thailand, Taiwan, Japan, Korea and China (SOTUS, 2gether, Bad Buddy, Cherry Magic!, Word of Honor). [aka BL drama; Y series; danmei drama]

C3-4 Nordic Noir: Scandinavian crime series of bleak landscapes and slow investigations (The Killing, The Bridge, Wallander, Trapped). [aka Scandinavian noir; Scandi noir]
C3-5 Spy & Espionage Series: series of intelligence agents (Mission: Impossible, The Americans, Homeland, Slow Horses, The Bureau). [aka spy series]

### C4 Comedy Series — Axis: comic form (multi-camera sitcom, single-camera, sketch, mockumentary).

C4-2 Single-Camera Comedy: film-style comedy series without laugh track (Curb Your Enthusiasm, Arrested Development, Fleabag, Atlanta). [aka single-camera sitcom]
C4-3 Mockumentary Sitcom: comedy in documentary form (The Office, Parks and Recreation, What We Do in the Shadows, Abbott Elementary). [aka mockumentary series]
C4-4 Sketch Comedy: series of comic sketches (Monty Python's Flying Circus, Saturday Night Live, Key & Peele). [aka sketch show]

C4-6 Satire & Political Comedy: comedy that satirises politics and institutions (Yes Minister, The Thick of It, Veep). [aka political satire]

## D Serial Drama Traditions — test: a long-running serial in a named serial tradition, with continuing storylines broadcast daily or weekly for years or hundreds of episodes: soap opera, telenovela, dizi, Indian daily soap, teleserye, musalsal (TV2).
  key: 1. Latin American or Iberian telenovela? → D1. 2. Anglophone soap opera? → D2. 3. Turkish dizi? → D3. 4. South Asian serial? → D4. 5. Arab musalsal? → D5. 6. Philippine teleserye? → D6.
### D1 Telenovela — Axis: sub-genre and industry tradition.
D1-1 Telenovela: the Latin American serial of a single romance resolved in one run of 100–200 episodes, Mexican, Venezuelan and Colombian (Los ricos también lloran, Yo soy Betty, la fea, Café con aroma de mujer). [aka novela; culebrón]
D1-2 Brazilian Novela: the Brazilian telenovela, above all TV Globo's prime-time novelas das nove (Roque Santeiro, Avenida Brasil, Escrava Isaura). [aka novela das nove; telenovela brasileira]
D1-3 Narconovela: the Colombian and Mexican telenovela of drug traffickers (El Patrón del Mal, La Reina del Sur, Sin senos no hay paraíso). [aka narco-telenovela]
D1-4 Youth Telenovela: telenovelas for children and teens (Chiquititas, Rebelde Way, Rebelde, Violetta). [aka juvenile telenovela]
### D2 Soap Opera — Axis: daypart and national industry (the field's own division).
D2-1 American Daytime Soap Opera: US daytime serials sponsored for the home audience (Guiding Light, General Hospital, Days of Our Lives, The Young and the Restless). [aka daytime soap]
D2-2 British & Irish Soap Opera: British and Irish continuing dramas of a community (Coronation Street, EastEnders, Emmerdale, Fair City). [aka continuing drama]
D2-3 Australian Soap Opera: Australian serials (Neighbours, Home and Away, Prisoner). [aka Aussie soap]
D2-4 Prime-Time Soap Opera: evening serials of wealthy families, intrigue and cliffhangers (Peyton Place, Dallas, Dynasty, Melrose Place, Desperate Housewives). [aka prime-time serial]
D2-5 European Soap Opera: the continental European daily and weekly soaps (Lindenstraße, Gute Zeiten, schlechte Zeiten, Un posto al sole, Goede tijden, slechte tijden). [aka ]
### D3 Dizi — Axis: setting.
D3-1 Dizi: the Turkish weekly serial of 120–150-minute episodes, romance, family honour and class (Aşk-ı Memnu, Fatmagül'ün Suçu Ne?, Kara Sevda). [aka Turkish drama; Turkish television drama]
D3-2 Historical Dizi: Turkish serials of the Ottoman and Seljuk past (Muhteşem Yüzyıl, Diriliş: Ertuğrul, Kuruluş: Osman). [aka Ottoman dizi]
### D4 South Asian Serials — Axis: serial genre.
D4-1 Indian Daily Soap: the Hindi and regional daily serial of the joint family, above all the saas-bahu (mother-in-law and daughter-in-law) serial of the 2000s (Kyunki Saas Bhi Kabhi Bahu Thi, Kahaani Ghar Ghar Kii, Balika Vadhu, Anupamaa). [aka saas-bahu serial; Indian soap opera]
D4-2 Indian Mythological Serial: serials of the epics and the gods (Ramayan, Mahabharat, Devon Ke Dev...Mahadev). [aka mythological serial]
D4-3 Pakistani Drama Serial: the Urdu prime-time drama serial of family and romance (Humsafar, Zindagi Gulzar Hai, Mere Paas Tum Ho). [aka Pakistani drama]
### D5 Musalsal — Axis: serial genre.
D5-1 Ramadan Musalsal: the Arab serial made for the Ramadan season, Syrian, Egyptian and Gulf (Bab al-Hara, Al-Taghriba al-Filistiniya, Omar). [aka musalsal; Ramadan series]
### D6 Teleserye — Axis: none (one leaf).
D6-1 Teleserye: the Philippine prime-time serial (Pangako Sa 'Yo, Ang Probinsyano, Encantadia). [aka teledrama; Filipino soap opera]

## G Unscripted Formats — test: an unscripted entertainment format licensed across borders (≥3 versions in ≥2 countries), with its national versions as variants; and the East Asian variety shows of the drama database.
  key: 1. Contestants compete for a prize or title? → G1 Competition Formats.
  key: 2. Real people filmed in arranged situations (dating, makeover, swaps, docusoap)? → G2 Factual Entertainment Formats.
  key: 3. Hosts, guests, games and talk in a studio or on location? → G3 Variety & Talk.
### G1 Competition Formats — Axis: what is contested.
G1-1 Talent Show: singing, dancing and variety talent contests judged by panel and public (Idols, Got Talent, The Voice, The X Factor, Dancing with the Stars). [aka talent competition]
G1-2 Reality Competition: contestants live together or travel and are eliminated (Survivor, Big Brother, The Amazing Race, Fort Boyard). [aka reality game show]
G1-3 Cooking Competition: contests of cooks and bakers (MasterChef, Hell's Kitchen, The Great British Bake Off, Top Chef). [aka cooking show]
G1-4 Game Show: quiz and game formats played in a studio (Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?, Wheel of Fortune, The Price Is Right, The Chase). [aka quiz show]
G1-5 Business Pitch Show: entrepreneurs pitch to investors (Dragons' Den, Shark Tank, The Apprentice). [aka ]

G2-1 Dating Show: formats of courtship and matchmaking (The Dating Game, The Bachelor, Love Island, Married at First Sight). [aka dating game show]
G2-2 Docusoap & Social Experiment: real people followed or placed in arranged situations (Wife Swap, The Real Housewives, Undercover Boss, Gogglebox). [aka docusoap; social experiment]

PRECEDENCE
F1 Named cycle before movement: A film with a home in a named genre cycle (chanbara, kaidan, J-horror, yakuza, film noir, pink film, Roman Porno, pinky violence, kaiju, tokusatsu, Taiyōzoku) goes to that cycle, even when a movement's reference sources list it as a member. A movement leaf keeps the attested members with no cycle home; every attested member carries the movement as a tag (D1).
F2 Named cycle before general genre: A named cycle with its own canon (J-horror, kaiju eiga, yakuza eiga, pink film) beats the general genre leaf (supernatural horror, monster film, gangster film, sexploitation).
F10 Leaf examples follow the key: When a film named as a leaf example would be sent elsewhere by the key, the key wins and the example is replaced; examples never override the key.
AN1 Battle before world: A series whose episodes are built on escalating fights, rivals and power systems (shōnen battle) is E1 even when set in a fantasy world (Fullmetal Alchemist, Demon Slayer, Jujutsu Kaisen); an isekai or a fantasy whose world and journey carry it stays E3 (Re:Zero, Frieren).
AN2 Franchise unity: All seasons, films, OVAs and specials of one franchise share the leaf of its main series, unless a spin-off belongs to another genre outright (a gag spin-off → E10).
AN3 Demographic: Shōnen, seinen, shōjo and josei are tags on every title (MAL/AniList demographic); they divide leaves only where the field names a demographic genre (shōjo romance, josei).
AN5 Comedy first: A series whose premise is a pretext for gags or parody (gag manga, a genre parody) is E10 whatever its setting or club (Detroit Metal City, Dragon Half, KonoSuba excepted as isekai, since its world carries the story).
AN6 Children's franchises: A children's franchise (Pokémon, Digimon, Doraemon) stays in E11 across its entries (AN2), including films made for its grown-up fans.
AN7 What carries the story: A setting, sport, band or speculative frame that is only a backdrop does not decide the leaf: the sport must drive the plot for E7 (Blue Box → romance), a fantasy world must carry the story for E3 (a villainess romance in a fantasy court → romance). Toy-line battle shows for children (Beyblade, Bakugan) are E11; one-off comedy specials and chibi extras of a franchise are E10.
AN8 Adult flag: E12 holds explicit hentai only; an adult-flagged art film or erotic drama (Cleopatra 1970, Shoujo Tsubaki) is placed by its genre with E12-1 as see-also. A gag series about everyday life is E10 (comedy first, AN5); a gentle everyday series without gag structure is E6.
AN9 Battle structure in any setting: AN1 holds for science-fiction and samurai settings too: a series built on escalating fights and power systems is E1 (Kaiju No. 8, Jigokuraku); a death game or gambling series whose engine is the game, not the fights, is E8-6 (Kakegurui, Juuni Taisen excepted as battle). Ecchi series whose fan service carries them stay E10-2 even with fights (High School DxD).
AN10 Frames and parodies: A magical-girl or robot frame on a romance or comedy does not make E4 or E2 (AN7); a magical-girl parody is E10. A historical war epic whose era and campaigns are the subject is E8-4 even with constant battles (Kingdom, Vinland Saga). Comedy shorts, bloopers and chibi extras of a franchise are E10; side stories in the franchise's own genre stay with it (AN2).
AN4 Non-Japanese animation in the anime database: Donghua and Korean animation selected through the anime database are placed by the same key, with country as a filter; animation from elsewhere selected as film is A15.
TV1 Animation: Every animated series that is not anime is C6, whatever its genre (The Simpsons → Animated Sitcom; Arcane → Animated Action-Adventure).
TV2 Serial or series: A long-running serial in a named serial tradition (telenovela, dizi, daily soap, soap opera, teleserye, musalsal) is D, not C, even when it is also a crime or historical story; a limited or seasonal series with a closed arc is C.
TV3 Named Asian tradition before genre: An Asian drama in a named tradition of C2 stays there whatever its genre mix (a sageuk with zombies, Kingdom, is Sageuk; a xianxia romance is Wuxia & Xianxia Drama); a Korean thriller or a Japanese medical drama with no tradition home goes by genre.
TV4 Crime before drama: A series centred on crime, police, lawyers defending criminals or spies is C3, even when its prestige makes it 'drama' (The Sopranos, The Wire, Breaking Bad); a legal series about the law firm's own life is Legal Drama.
TV6 Daily comedies, crime comedies and true events: A daily or long-running comedy is a sitcom (C4), not a serial tradition, even with thousands of episodes (Taarak Mehta Ka Ooltah Chashmah). A comedy about criminals goes by laughter first (C4). A dramatised real crime is True Crime Drama (C3-7); a dramatised real event that is not a crime is Docudrama Series (C1-9).
TV7 Non-fiction: A documentary series is C8; a talk, chat or variety programme that is not a cross-border format goes to G3 (variety and talk) as a programme of its own.
TV5 Franchise unity: Spin-offs and revivals share their parent's leaf unless they change genre outright.
