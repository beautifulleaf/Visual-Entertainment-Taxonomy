1. Is it a building or a designed built whole (a garden, a complex), placed by its style? → K Architecture. Ornament carved, painted or tiled on a building and studied where it stands is asked next (2); loose architectural fragments in a museum go by their material (D, E, I).
2. Is it made on or into a fixed surface and studied where it stands: rock art, wall painting, in-situ mosaic and tile revetment, a geoglyph, or art on the living body? → L Art in Place.
3. Is it a photograph or made with a camera? → J Photography. Is it time-based, installed or performed (video, installation, performance, digital)? → M New Media, Installation & Performance.
4. Is the written or decorated book the work: calligraphy, illumination (ornament), binding, paper decoration, or a manuscript tradition studied as the book? → C Book Arts & Calligraphy. Pictures in a manuscript belong to a painting tradition and go on to 6 (precedence P1).
5. Is it a drawing made as an end in itself or as a design, or a print pulled from a matrix? → B Drawings & Prints.
6. Is it a picture made with paint or ink on a movable flat support (paper, vellum, panel, canvas, cloth, silk)? → A Painting.
7. Is it carved, modelled or cast as an image or as carving for its own sake: statues, reliefs, carved stucco, ivory, bone, jade, rock crystal and hardstone, masks and puppets? → D Sculpture & Carving. A vessel carved from rock crystal or jade stays here; a cast metal vessel is G.
8. Otherwise place it by its material: fired clay → E Ceramics; glass → F Glass; metal (vessels, instruments, arms, jewellery) → G Metalwork, Arms & Jewellery; fibre (carpets, woven, embroidered, dyed and felted textiles, dress) → H Textiles, Carpets & Dress; wood, lacquer and papier-mâché, furniture → I Woodwork, Lacquer & Furniture.

PRECEDENCE
P1 Manuscript painting vs book arts: Pictures painted in a manuscript or for an album go to A1 Manuscript & Album Painting when the tradition is studied as painting (Persian, Ottoman, Mughal, Deccan, Arab); the book as an object — its script, illumination ornament and binding — goes to C. An illustrated manuscript is placed by what makes it notable: its pictures → A1, its script or illumination → C.
P2 Tiles: ware vs revetment: A tile is placed by its ware in E Ceramics (Iznik tiles under Iznik Pottery; Kashan lustre tiles under Kashan Lustreware). A revetment assembled on site as a named technique — cut-tile mosaic, zellige, kashi kari, mirror mosaic — is L3, because the work is the wall.
P3 Architectural carving: building vs fragment: Carved stucco, stone and wood that survive as museum fragments go to their material family (D1 carving, I1 woodwork). The building that carries them is K; a named in-situ programme of painting or mosaic is L.
P4 Lustre across bodies: Lustre is a surface technique, not a sub-family: a lustreware is placed by its body (earthenware lustre → E1; fritware lustre → E2) and then by its named ware.
P5 Carved hardstone vessels: A vessel carved from rock crystal, jade or other hardstone is D3 (the carving is the work), not glass or metalwork, even when mounted in metal.
P6 Dynasty is a tag: A dynasty, reign, empire or period (Mamluk, Safavid, Qajar, Akbar's reign) is a period tag, never a node. The node is the tradition the work belongs to in its medium: Mamluk enamelled glass (F3), Mamluk metalwork (G1), Mamluk carpets (H1), Mamluk architecture (K).
P7 Place is a tag: A city, production centre, country or culture label (Iznik, Kashan, Tabriz, 'Iranian', 'Syrian') is a place tag. A centre becomes a kind under a leaf only when the field names a distinct school or ware after it (Herat school, Kashan lustreware, Ushak carpets).
P8 Revivals: A revival built outside the original tradition (Moorish Revival synagogues, Neo-Mamluk, the First National Architectural Movement) is a leaf in K Historicist & National Revivals, never a kind of the style it revives.
P9 Makers: A named maker is a kind under the leaf of the tradition they worked in, linked to their school or atelier where one is recorded. A maker active in two traditions is placed where most of their recorded work sits, with a see-also to the other (Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd al-Samad: Persian schooling, Mughal atelier → Mughal Painting).
P10 Folk and court: A folk or vernacular craft that the field names on its own (suzani, kilim, Berber jewellery) is its own leaf in the medium family; a court or urban tradition of the same medium is a separate leaf. Garment types (abaya, shemagh, ghutra) are not art traditions: they belong to the Possession Taxonomy.
P11 Period style before region: In artist-led cells a painter, sculptor or printmaker goes to the movement or school their work is attested in; with no attested movement, to the period-style leaf of their medium (Baroque Painting, Romantic Painting ...), never to a country.
P12 Tradition per medium: A culture is never a leaf by itself: its leaves are its traditions in each medium (Yoruba sculpture, Yoruba beadwork), so one culture can hold several leaves across families. A culture known for one medium only gets one leaf there.
P13 Archaeological cultures: An archaeological culture known for one dominant medium (Nok terracottas, Jōmon pottery) is a leaf in that medium; its other finds are kinds or see-also, not separate leaves, unless the field names them.

MODIFIERS (tags and filters, never nodes)
- Period and dynasty — a filter on every kind and leaf (Umayyad, Abbasid, Fatimid, Seljuq, Ilkhanid, Mamluk, Timurid, Safavid, Ottoman, Mughal, Qajar ...). Never a node (P6).
- Region and place — the 20-region grid of the gathering, plus production centre and country as tags (P7).
- Ornament and motif — girih (geometric strapwork), arabesque (islimi), muqarnas, saz leaves, cintamani: a tag across media, never a leaf, since one motif runs through tiles, carpets, metalwork and buildings (open question Q6).
- Holding institution and collection — evidence, never a node.
- Patron and reign — a tag (Akbar, Shah Tahmasp, Süleyman); a royal atelier is a kind only when it is a recorded workshop (Imperial Mughal atelier, the Ottoman nakkaşhane).
- Object type — bowl, ewer, tile, folio, page: an attribute of representative works, not a node. A leaf is a tradition, not an object type, except where the field treats the type as a tradition (astrolabes).
- Inscription and script on objects — an attribute; calligraphy as an art (C1) is placed by script.
