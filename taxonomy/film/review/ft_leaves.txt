TAB KEY
1. Is it Japanese animation (anime), in any format — TV series, film, OVA, ONA, special? → E Anime; format is a filter (D4). Animation from elsewhere is placed by its form below (a feature → A15 Animated Feature).
2. Is it an unscripted format licensed across countries (talent competition, dating, cooking, game show, docusoap)? → G Unscripted Formats.
3. Is it a long-running serial in a named serial tradition — telenovela, dizi, Indian daily soap, soap opera? → D Serial Drama Traditions.
4. Any other scripted series or miniseries, from anywhere — including the East and Southeast Asian drama traditions (K-drama, Chinese costume drama, asadora, taiga drama, lakorn)? → C Television Series (D3: Asian drama is a filter, not a family).
5. A film of 40 minutes or less? → B Short Film. Otherwise → A Feature Film (the Academy's 40-minute line).

## A Feature Film — test: a film of more than 40 minutes made as a single work for cinema, festival, television or streaming release; not anime.
  key: 1. Non-fiction (documentary, concert film, nature film, essay film)? → A13 Documentary.
  key: 2. Animated, and not Japanese animation? → A15 Animated Feature (F17).
  key: 3. Does it belong to a named genre cycle — a narrow leaf of A2–A12, A17 or A18 (chanbara, kaidan, J-horror, yakuza, film noir, giallo, poliziottesco, blaxploitation, heroic bloodshed, wuxia, spaghetti western, pink film, kaiju, masala film ...)? → that leaf (F1, F2, F13). Otherwise, is it an attested member of a named film movement (Italian neorealism, the French New Wave, New Hollywood ...)? → A1 Movements & Schools.
  key: 4. Avant-garde, working outside narrative convention (abstract, structural, surrealist)? → A16 Experimental & Avant-Garde.
  key: 5. Is erotic display or sexual obsession the film's reason to exist? → A12 Erotic Film. A violent exploitation cycle with erotic elements is not: it goes on.
  key: 6. Does it aim first to frighten (ghosts, monsters, killers, gore)? → A4 Horror (F3).
  key: 7. Superheroes? → A6-3 (F15). Speculative science — the future, technology, space, aliens, giant monsters? → A5 Science Fiction & Monster (F5). Magic, myth, fairy tale or a quest in perilous places? → A6 Fantasy & Adventure.
  key: 8. The American frontier? → A17 Western (F20). Set in a past era that defines it, a real life, or about war? → A7 Historical, Period & War (F4, F11, F14).
  key: 9. Made first for children, or built on Christmas? → A19 Children's & Family (F18).
  key: 10. Espionage? → A3-7 Spy Film (F16). Criminals or police at the centre? → A2 Crime. Suspense, conspiracy or puzzle? → A3 Thriller & Mystery (F6).
  key: 11. Fights, chases and stunts carry it? → A8 Action & Martial Arts.
  key: 12. Songs or dance carry the story? → A11 Musical & Music. Laughter first? → A10 Comedy. A love story? → A9 Romance. Otherwise → A14 Drama (F7, F19).
### A1 Movements & Schools — Axis: the named movement (membership attested in its reference sources).
A1-1 Japanese New Wave: the Nūberu bāgu: films of Ōshima, Imamura, Yoshida, Shinoda, Teshigahara, Hani and their peers (c. 1959–1975) that broke with studio humanism through politics, sexuality and formal rupture (Cruel Story of Youth, Woman in the Dunes, Death by Hanging). Members with a genre-cycle home (Harakiri, Kwaidan, Branded to Kill) sit in that cycle and carry the movement as a tag (D1). [aka Nūberu bāgu; Shōchiku Nouvelle Vague]
A1-2 German Expressionism: the Weimar film movement of distorted sets, chiaroscuro and haunted protagonists, c. 1919–1931 (The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari, Nosferatu, Metropolis, M). [aka Expressionist film; Weimar cinema]
A1-3 Soviet Montage: the Soviet cinema of the 1920s built on montage theory — Eisenstein, Pudovkin, Vertov, Dovzhenko (Battleship Potemkin, Strike, Mother, Earth). [aka Soviet montage cinema; montage school]
A1-4 French Impressionist Cinema: the French avant-garde narrative cinema of the 1920s — Gance, L'Herbier, Epstein, Dulac — built on subjective camera and rhythmic cutting (La Roue, Coeur fidèle, The Fall of the House of Usher). [aka première vague; first French avant-garde]
A1-5 Poetic Realism: French films of the 1930s of doomed working-class lovers in fog-bound ports and back streets — Carné, Prévert, Renoir, Duvivier, Vigo (Port of Shadows, Le jour se lève, L'Atalante). [aka réalisme poétique]
A1-6 Italian Neorealism: Italian films of 1943–1952 shot on location with non-professional actors about the poor and the war's aftermath — Rossellini, De Sica, Visconti, early Fellini (Rome, Open City, Bicycle Thieves, La terra trema, Umberto D.). [aka neorealismo; Italian neo-realism]
A1-7 French New Wave: the Nouvelle Vague of 1958–1968: Cahiers critics turned directors — Godard, Truffaut, Chabrol, Rohmer, Rivette, Varda — with location shooting, jump cuts and personal subjects (The 400 Blows, Breathless, Jules and Jim, Cléo from 5 to 7). [aka Nouvelle Vague]
A1-8 Left Bank: the Rive Gauche group beside the New Wave — Resnais, Marker, Varda, Robbe-Grillet, Duras — literary, political and formally experimental (Hiroshima mon amour, Last Year at Marienbad). [aka Rive Gauche]
A1-9 British New Wave: British films of 1959–1963 of working-class life in the industrial North, from the Angry Young Men and kitchen-sink writers (Saturday Night and Sunday Morning, A Taste of Honey, This Sporting Life). [aka kitchen sink realism; British social realism]
A1-10 Czechoslovak New Wave: Czech and Slovak films of 1963–1969 — Forman, Chytilová, Menzel, Němec, Jireš — absurdist, satirical and humane (Daisies, Closely Watched Trains, The Firemen's Ball, Marketa Lazarová). [aka Czech New Wave; Československá nová vlna]
A1-11 Polish Film School: Polish films of 1956–1963 about the war and its moral wreckage — Wajda, Munk, Kawalerowicz, Has (Kanał, Ashes and Diamonds, Eroica). [aka Polska Szkoła Filmowa]
A1-12 Yugoslav Black Wave: the critical, dark and sexually frank Yugoslav films of 1963–1972 — Makavejev, Petrović, Žilnik, Pavlović (WR: Mysteries of the Organism, I Even Met Happy Gypsies). [aka Crni talas]
A1-13 Cinema Novo: the Brazilian movement of the 1960s–70s — Glauber Rocha, Nelson Pereira dos Santos, Joaquim Pedro de Andrade — 'an idea in the head and a camera in the hand' (Black God, White Devil, Vidas Secas, Entranced Earth, Macunaíma). [aka Cinema Novo]
A1-14 Third Cinema: the Latin American and Third World militant cinema of the late 1960s–70s set against Hollywood and auteur cinema (The Hour of the Furnaces, Memories of Underdevelopment, The Battle of Algiers). [aka Tercer Cine; Third World cinema]
A1-15 New Hollywood: the American director-driven cinema of c. 1967–1980 — Penn, Altman, Coppola, Scorsese, Ashby, Malick — in the wake of the studio system (Bonnie and Clyde, Easy Rider, Five Easy Pieces, Nashville). [aka American New Wave; Hollywood Renaissance]
A1-16 New German Cinema: West German auteur cinema of 1962–1982 after the Oberhausen Manifesto — Fassbinder, Herzog, Wenders, Schlöndorff, von Trotta, Kluge (Aguirre, the Wrath of God, Ali: Fear Eats the Soul, Kings of the Road). [aka Neuer Deutscher Film; Young German Cinema]
A1-17 Parallel Cinema: Indian art cinema from the 1950s — Satyajit Ray, Ritwik Ghatak, Mrinal Sen, Adoor Gopalakrishnan, Shyam Benegal — realist and outside the song-and-dance mainstream (Pather Panchali, The Cloud-Capped Star, Ankur). [aka Indian New Wave; art cinema (India)]
A1-18 Iranian New Wave: Iranian art cinema from the late 1960s and its post-revolutionary flowering — Kiarostami, Makhmalbaf, Panahi, Majidi — with child protagonists, non-actors and fiction-documentary blends (The Cow, Close-Up, Taste of Cherry, Children of Heaven). [aka Iranian New Wave cinema; Moj-e No]
A1-19 Taiwan New Cinema: the Taiwanese New Wave of 1982–1990 — Hou Hsiao-hsien, Edward Yang — long takes and everyday history (A City of Sadness, A Brighter Summer Day, The Time to Live and the Time to Die). [aka Taiwanese New Wave]
A1-20 Hong Kong New Wave: the Hong Kong directors of 1979–1990 trained in television — Tsui Hark, Ann Hui, Patrick Tam, Allen Fong — and the 'second wave' of Wong Kar-wai and Stanley Kwan (Boat People, Days of Being Wild, Rouge). [aka HK New Wave; Hong Kong second wave]
A1-21 Fifth Generation: the first class after the Cultural Revolution at the Beijing Film Academy — Chen Kaige, Zhang Yimou, Tian Zhuangzhuang — allegorical rural epics of the 1980s–90s (Yellow Earth, Red Sorghum, Raise the Red Lantern, Farewell My Concubine). [aka Chinese Fifth Generation]
A1-22 Sixth Generation: Chinese independent urban realism of the 1990s–2000s — Jia Zhangke, Wang Xiaoshuai, Lou Ye, Zhang Yuan (Platform, Beijing Bicycle, Suzhou River, Still Life). [aka Urban Generation]
A1-23 Dogme 95: the Danish manifesto films made under the 'Vow of Chastity' — hand-held, location sound, no effects (The Celebration, The Idiots, Italian for Beginners). [aka Dogma 95]
A1-24 Romanian New Wave: Romanian minimalist realism from the 2000s — Puiu, Mungiu, Porumboiu, Muntean — long takes of bureaucracy and the moral afterlife of communism (The Death of Mr. Lazarescu, 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days, 12:08 East of Bucharest). [aka Noul val românesc]
A1-25 Berlin School: the German art cinema from the late 1990s — Petzold, Arslan, Schanelec, Ade, Köhler — detached, precise and observational (Barbara, Toni Erdmann, Phoenix). [aka Berliner Schule]
A1-26 New Queer Cinema: the independent queer films of the early 1990s named by B. Ruby Rich — Haynes, Araki, Van Sant, Kalin, Jarman (Poison, My Own Private Idaho, The Living End, Paris Is Burning as documentary). [aka Queer New Wave]
A1-27 Mumblecore: American micro-budget films of the 2000s with naturalistic acting and improvised dialogue about twenty-somethings — the Duplass brothers, Swanberg, Bujalski, early Gerwig (Funny Ha Ha, Hannah Takes the Stairs, The Puffy Chair). [aka bedhead cinema]
A1-28 L.A. Rebellion: the Black filmmakers of UCLA, late 1960s–1980s — Charles Burnett, Haile Gerima, Julie Dash, Billy Woodberry (Killer of Sheep, Bush Mama, Daughters of the Dust). [aka Los Angeles School of Black Filmmakers]
A1-29 Australian New Wave: the revival of Australian feature production c. 1970–1985 — Weir, Armstrong, Beresford, Schepisi (Picnic at Hanging Rock, Walkabout, Breaker Morant, My Brilliant Career). [aka Australian film revival]
A1-30 Greek Weird Wave: Greek films of the late 2000s–2010s — Lanthimos, Tsangari, Avranas — deadpan, absurd and alienated (Dogtooth, Attenberg, Alps). [aka Greek New Wave]
A1-31 New Argentine Cinema: Argentine independent cinema from the mid-1990s — Martel, Trapero, Caetano, Alonso (Pizza, Beer, and Cigarettes, La Ciénaga, Nine Queens excepted as heist). [aka Nuevo cine argentino]
A1-32 Cinéma du look: French films of the 1980s with a glossy, youthful style from advertising and music video — Beineix, Besson, Carax (Diva, Subway, The Lovers on the Bridge, Betty Blue). [aka ]
A1-33 Slow Cinema: contemplative art cinema of long takes, minimal story and observed duration, as named by critics from the 2000s: films of its core practitioners in that mode — Tarr, Tsai Ming-liang, Lav Diaz, Weerasethakul, Alonso, Reygadas, Costa, Ceylan, Angelopoulos — and Jeanne Dielman (Sátántangó, Uncle Boonmee Who Can Recall His Past Lives). Precursors (Tarkovsky, Bresson, Ozu, Antonioni) carry it as see-also only; a film of a core director whose own genre is clear goes there. [aka contemplative cinema; cinema of slowness]
A1-35 Cinema Marginal: the Brazilian underground of 1968–1973 that broke with Cinema Novo — Sganzerla, Bressane, Reichenbach (The Red Light Bandit, Killed the Family and Went to the Movies). [aka Cinema de Boca do Lixo; Udigrudi]
A1-34 Korean New Wave: the socially critical South Korean films of the 1980s–90s after the end of military censorship — Park Kwang-su, Jang Sun-woo, Lee Chang-dong's debut (Chilsu and Mansu, A Single Spark, Green Fish). [aka Korean New Cinema]
A1-40 No Wave Cinema: the underground film scene of downtown New York, c. 1976–1985, beside No Wave music — Amos Poe, Beth B, Vivienne Dick, early Jarmusch (Smithereens, Born in Flames, Stranger Than Paradise, Permanent Vacation). [aka No Wave film]
A1-37 Cinema of Moral Anxiety: Polish films of 1976–1981 on moral compromise under late communism — Kieślowski, Zanussi, Holland, Wajda (Camera Buff, Man of Marble, Camouflage). [aka Kino moralnego niepokoju]
A1-38 Portuguese New Cinema: the Novo Cinema of the 1960s–70s that broke with the Estado Novo's studio films — Paulo Rocha, Fernando Lopes, António de Macedo (The Green Years, Change of Life, Belarmino). [aka Novo Cinema; Cinema Novo (Portugal)]
A1-39 Second Golden Age of Philippine Cinema: the Filipino auteur cinema of the 1970s–early 80s under martial law — Lino Brocka, Ishmael Bernal, Mike de Leon (Manila in the Claws of Light, Insiang, Himala). [aka Philippine New Wave (1970s)]
A1-36 New American Cinema: the American independent and underground film movement of the late 1950s–60s around the New American Cinema Group and the Film-Makers' Cooperative — Cassavetes, Frank, Mekas, Clarke (Shadows, Pull My Daisy, The Connection). [aka New American Cinema Group; American underground film]
### A2 Crime — Axis: whose side the story takes and its milieu.
A2-1 Yakuza Film: yakuza eiga: films of the Japanese gangster world, from the chivalrous ninkyō films of 1960s Toei to the documentary-style jitsuroku films (Battles Without Honor and Humanity) and the V-cinema and Kitano cycles (Sonatine). [aka yakuza eiga; ninkyō eiga; jitsuroku eiga]
A2-2 Gangster Film: films centred on organised criminals and their rise and fall outside the named national cycles. [aka mob film]
A2-3 Police & Detective Film: films centred on police or detectives working a case (Stray Dog, The French Connection, Memories of Murder); buddy-cop films are A8-7. [aka police procedural; detective film]
A2-4 Crime Drama: films about crime, its perpetrators and its effects, told as drama rather than suspense or gang epic (Vengeance Is Mine, Dog Day Afternoon, City of God). [aka crime film]
A2-5 Film Noir & Neo-Noir: crime films in the noir manner — fatalism, low-key style, corrupt cities, the femme fatale: the American cycle of the 1940s–50s (Double Indemnity, The Big Sleep) and its later neo-noir and national forms (Chinatown, Pale Flower, Branded to Kill). [aka noir; neo-noir]
A2-6 Pinky Violence: Toei's 1970s cycle of women's revenge, delinquent-girl (sukeban) and women-in-prison films, with violence and eroticism (Female Prisoner #701: Scorpion). [aka sukeban films; women-in-prison film]
A2-7 Heist Film: films built on planning and pulling off a robbery, and on its unravelling (The Italian Job, Heat, Ocean's Eleven, Reservoir Dogs; noir heists such as The Asphalt Jungle and Rififi are Film Noir by F1). [aka caper film; heist movie]
A2-8 Prison Film: films set inside prisons and built on incarceration, its society and escape (A Man Escaped, Escape from Alcatraz, The Shawshank Redemption, A Prophet). [aka prison drama; escape film]
A2-9 Poliziottesco: the Italian crime cycle of the 1970s — vigilante police, kidnapping and urban violence (Almost Human, The Big Racket, Rome Armed to the Teeth). [aka poliziotteschi; Euro-crime]
A2-10 Blaxploitation: the American cycle of 1970s films with Black heroes for Black urban audiences, mostly crime and action (Shaft, Super Fly, Coffy, Foxy Brown). [aka blaxploitation film]
A2-12 Cine Quinqui: the Spanish cycle of 1977–1985 about juvenile delinquents of the urban periphery, often played by non-actors (Deprisa, deprisa, Navajeros, El Pico). [aka cinema quinqui]
A2-11 Hood Film: American films of the 1990s and after about young Black and Latino life in inner-city neighbourhoods, gangs and violence (Boyz n the Hood, Menace II Society, Juice). [aka 'hood film; ghetto film]
### A3 Thriller & Mystery — Axis: the source of suspense.
A3-1 Psychological Thriller: suspense built on disturbed minds, obsession and manipulation (Cure, Gone Girl, Black Swan). [aka ]
A3-2 Mystery Film: whodunits and puzzle films built on detection, including honkaku adaptations and Agatha Christie films (The Inugamis, Knives Out). [aka whodunit; detective mystery]
A3-3 Survival & Death-Game Thriller: films in which characters are forced into a lethal contest or a fight to survive an ordeal (Battle Royale, The Hunger Games, 127 Hours, Cast Away). [aka death game; survival thriller]
A3-4 Erotic Thriller: suspense driven by sexual desire, seduction and danger, the Hollywood cycle of the 1980s–90s and its heirs (Fatal Attraction, Basic Instinct, Body Heat, Decision to Leave). [aka ]
A3-5 Revenge Thriller: suspense built on a protagonist's revenge, including rape-and-revenge and the Korean revenge cycle (Oldboy, Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance, I Saw the Devil, Ms .45). [aka revenge film; rape and revenge]
A3-6 Political & Conspiracy Thriller: suspense built on state power, political violence, cover-ups and paranoia (Z, The Parallax View, All the President's Men, The Lives of Others). [aka political thriller; paranoid thriller; conspiracy film]
A3-7 Spy Film: films whose plot is driven by espionage, from the Bond cycle to Cold War realism (Dr. No, The Spy Who Came in from the Cold, Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy, Mission: Impossible) (F16). [aka espionage film; spy thriller]
A3-8 Giallo: the Italian thriller-horror cycle of the 1960s–70s: black-gloved killers, stylised murders and amateur sleuths (Blood and Black Lace, The Bird with the Crystal Plumage, Deep Red). [aka gialli; giallo film]
A3-9 Suspense Thriller: thrillers of an ordinary protagonist in danger — the wrong man, the stalker, the hostage, the trap — outside the narrower leaves (Rear Window, North by Northwest, Duel, Panic Room). [aka thriller film; Hitchcockian thriller]
### A4 Horror — Axis: the source of fear.
A4-1 J-Horror: the Japanese horror cycle of the late 1990s–2000s: vengeful onryō, curses carried by media and technology, slow dread (Ring, Ju-On, Pulse, Dark Water). [aka Japanese horror; J-horror cycle]
A4-2 Kaidan Film: kaidan eiga: ghost-story films drawn from Edo and folk tales (Ugetsu, Kwaidan, Onibaba, Kuroneko). [aka kaidan eiga; Japanese ghost story film]
A4-3 Supernatural Horror: horror from ghosts, demons, possession and curses outside the named cycles (The Exorcist, Hereditary, House). [aka horror movie; scary movie]
A4-4 Body Horror: horror of the body transformed, invaded or fused with machines (Tetsuo: The Iron Man). [aka biological horror]
A4-5 Splatter & Gore Film: horror built on graphic violence, mutilation and torture (Audition, Ichi the Killer, Tokyo Gore Police). [aka splatter film; ero guro; extreme cinema]
A4-6 Zombie Film: horror of the walking dead (One Cut of the Dead, Versus). [aka ]
A4-7 Found-Footage Horror: horror presented as recovered amateur or documentary footage (Noroi: The Curse). Found-footage films that are not horror go by genre (Chronicle, Cloverfield). [aka found footage]
A4-8 Slasher Film: horror built on a killer stalking and murdering victims one by one (Evil Dead Trap). [aka slasher]
A4-9 Psychological Horror: horror whose dread comes from disturbed minds, unreality and inescapable spaces rather than monsters or gore (Strange Circus, Haze, Exit 8). [aka ]
A4-10 Folk Horror: horror rooted in rural isolation, pagan ritual and folklore (Witchfinder General, The Wicker Man, The Witch, Midsommar). [aka ]
A4-11 Vampire Film: horror and its offshoots built on vampires (Dracula 1931, Let the Right One In, Thirst, Near Dark). [aka vampire movie]
A4-12 Haunted House Film: horror of a haunted dwelling and its ghosts (The Haunting, The Innocents, The Shining, The Conjuring). [aka haunted house movie]
A4-13 Gothic Horror: horror of castles, curses and the literary monsters of the Gothic tradition: the Universal monster cycle, Hammer, the Corman–Poe cycle and Italian Gothic (Frankenstein, The Curse of Frankenstein, Black Sunday, Crimson Peak; Dracula films are A4-11). [aka Hammer horror; Universal monsters; Italian gothic]
A4-14 Creature Feature & Natural Horror: horror of monsters, mutated beasts and nature turned killer, outside the giant kaiju (Jaws, The Birds, Tremors, Gremlins, An American Werewolf in London). [aka monster movie; natural horror; werewolf film; eco-horror]
A4-15 Science Fiction Horror: horror whose threat comes from science, space or aliens (Alien, The Thing, Invasion of the Body Snatchers, Event Horizon, The Fly). [aka sci-fi horror]
A4-16 Comedy Horror: horror played mainly for laughs, outside a named cycle (The Evil Dead II, What We Do in the Shadows, Tucker & Dale vs. Evil) (F12). [aka horror comedy]
A4-19 Jiangshi Film: the Hong Kong cycle of hopping-corpse films, horror played with kung fu and comedy (Mr. Vampire, Encounters of the Spooky Kind, Rigor Mortis). [aka hopping vampire film; geungsi film]
A4-20 Home Invasion Film: horror and thrillers of a household besieged by intruders (The Strangers, Funny Games, Don't Breathe, You're Next). [aka home invasion thriller; siege horror]
A4-17 Cannibal Film: the Italian exploitation cycle of the late 1970s–early 80s set among jungle cannibals (Cannibal Holocaust, Cannibal Ferox). [aka cannibal boom; Italian cannibal film]
A4-18 Asian Horror: the East and Southeast Asian supernatural horror cycles that followed J-horror — Thai, Korean, Indonesian, Malaysian, Taiwanese and Hong Kong ghost, possession and shamanic horror; as a named cycle it wins over the subgenre leaves (F2) (Shutter, A Tale of Two Sisters, Satan's Slaves, The Eye). [aka Asian horror; K-horror; Thai horror]
### A5 Science Fiction & Monster — Axis: the speculative premise.
A5-1 Kaiju Film: kaiju eiga and giant-monster films: Godzilla, Mothra, Gamera and their Hollywood and Korean kin (Godzilla 1954, King Kong, The Host, Pacific Rim). [aka kaiju eiga; giant monster film]
A5-2 Tokusatsu Hero Film: live-action special-effects films of costumed transforming heroes (Ultraman, Kamen Rider, Super Sentai). [aka tokusatsu; henshin hero]
A5-3 Science Fiction Film: speculative films of the future, technology, artificial minds and alien contact outside the narrower leaves (2001: A Space Odyssey, Arrival, Summer Time Machine Blues). [aka sci-fi]
A5-4 Cyberpunk: high-tech, low-life science fiction of cybernetic bodies and urban decay, with its Japanese punk-industrial strand (Tetsuo, Burst City). [aka Japanese cyberpunk]
A5-5 Disaster & Post-Apocalyptic Film: films of catastrophe — natural, technological or speculative — and of collapse and survival after it (Airport, The Poseidon Adventure, Japan Sinks, Mad Max: Fury Road) (F26). [aka disaster film; post-apocalyptic film]
A5-6 Space Opera & Space Adventure: adventure on an interplanetary scale: starships, galactic empires and space westerns (Star Wars, Guardians of the Galaxy, Dune). [aka space opera; space western]
A5-7 Dystopian Film: films of oppressive future societies (Metropolis excepted as Expressionism; Brazil, Children of Men, Snowpiercer, The Hunger Games excepted as survival). [aka dystopia]
A5-8 Alien Invasion & First Contact: films of extraterrestrial invasion or contact (The War of the Worlds, Close Encounters of the Third Kind, Independence Day, District 9). [aka alien invasion film]
A5-9 Time Travel Film: films whose plot is built on travel in time or time loops (Back to the Future, 12 Monkeys, Primer, Groundhog Day excepted as comedy). [aka time loop film]
### A6 Fantasy & Adventure — Axis: the kind of wonder.
A6-1 Fantasy Film: live-action films of magic, myth and invented worlds outside the narrower leaves (Harry Potter, Pan's Labyrinth, The Great Yokai War). [aka ]
A6-2 Adventure Film: journeys, quests and treasure hunts in exotic or perilous settings (Raiders of the Lost Ark, The Mummy 1999). [aka ]
A6-3 Superhero Film: films of costumed or super-powered heroes — the Marvel and DC cycles and their kin — and live-action adaptations of power-based manga (Superman, The Dark Knight, Gantz, Death Note) (F15). [aka superhero film; comic-book film; live-action manga adaptation]
A6-4 High Fantasy: epic fantasy of invented worlds, quests and sword-and-sorcery (The Lord of the Rings, Conan the Barbarian, The NeverEnding Story). [aka epic fantasy; sword and sorcery]
A6-5 Fairy-Tale Film: live-action films of fairy tales and their retellings (Beauty and the Beast 1946, The Princess Bride, Three Wishes for Cinderella). [aka fairy tale film]
A6-6 Swashbuckler: adventure of sword-fighting heroes — pirates, musketeers, outlaws (The Adventures of Robin Hood, The Three Musketeers, Pirates of the Caribbean). [aka swashbuckler film; cloak-and-dagger; pirate film]
### A7 Historical, Period & War — Axis: the era and what the past is used for.
A7-1 Jidaigeki: period drama set before the Meiji Restoration in which the past is the subject — manners, politics, family and fate rather than swordplay (Sansho the Bailiff, The Life of Oharu, Twilight Samurai). [aka period drama (Japanese); historical drama]
A7-2 Chanbara: samurai swordplay films in which sword combat carries the drama (Seven Samurai, Yojimbo, Zatoichi, 13 Assassins). [aka samurai film; swordplay film; chambara]
A7-3 War Film: films about war and soldiers' experience of it (Fires on the Plain, The Burmese Harp, The Human Condition). [aka anti-war film]
A7-4 Historical Drama: films whose past era defines them outside the narrower leaves: dramas of real events and of fictional lives in a recorded past, from the Meiji era to the twentieth century (The Story of the Last Chrysanthemum, Titanic, The Last Emperor); lives of real people are A7-8. [aka historical film; period film]
A7-5 Atomic Bomb Film: genbaku eiga: films about the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the hibakusha (Children of Hiroshima, Black Rain). [aka genbaku eiga; hibakusha cinema]
A7-6 Sword-and-Sandal Film: epics of the ancient and biblical world, including the Italian peplum (Ben-Hur, Spartacus, The Ten Commandments, Hercules 1958, Gladiator). [aka peplum; biblical epic]
A7-7 Costume Drama: dramas of manners in a period setting, often literary adaptations and heritage films (Barry Lyndon, The Age of Innocence, Pride & Prejudice, The Leopard). [aka period drama; heritage film]
A7-8 Biographical Film: films that tell a real person's life or career (Lawrence of Arabia, Gandhi, Amadeus, The Social Network) (F14). [aka biopic; biographical drama]
A7-9 Holocaust Film: fiction films about the Nazi persecution and murder of the Jews (Schindler's List, The Pianist, Son of Saul, The Zone of Interest). [aka ]
A7-10 Partisan Film: Yugoslav, Albanian and other Eastern-bloc films of the Second World War partisan resistance (The Battle of Neretva, Walter Defends Sarajevo, Red Poppies on the Wall; Come and See excepted as war). [aka partizanski film]
### A8 Action & Martial Arts — Axis: the fighting tradition.
A8-1 Martial Arts Film: films built on unarmed or weapons combat in modern or timeless settings outside chanbara, kung fu and wuxia (The Street Fighter, The Raid, Ong-Bak). [aka karate film]
A8-2 Action Film: films carried by fights, chases, gunplay and stunts outside the narrower leaves (Die Hard, Mad Max: Fury Road, John Wick). [aka action thriller]
A8-3 Wuxia: Chinese films of martial-arts chivalry: wandering swordsmen and women of the jianghu with gravity-defying combat (Come Drink with Me, A Touch of Zen, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, Hero). [aka wuxia pian; swordplay film]
A8-4 Kung Fu Film: Hong Kong and Chinese unarmed martial-arts films, from Shaw Brothers and Bruce Lee to Jackie Chan and Ip Man (Enter the Dragon, The 36th Chamber of Shaolin, Drunken Master, Ip Man). [aka kung fu movie; chopsocky]
A8-5 Heroic Bloodshed: the Hong Kong gunplay cycle of the late 1980s–90s: brotherhood, honour and balletic shoot-outs (A Better Tomorrow, The Killer, Hard Boiled). [aka gun fu]
A8-6 Hong Kong Action Cinema: Hong Kong stunt-driven action outside kung fu, wuxia and heroic bloodshed: police action, girls-with-guns and the Milkyway crime-action films (Police Story, Project A, Exiled). [aka Hong Kong action film]
A8-7 Buddy Cop Film: action films of two mismatched police partners (48 Hrs., Lethal Weapon, Rush Hour, Hot Fuzz). [aka buddy cop]
### A9 Romance — Axis: tone.
A9-1 Romantic Drama: love stories told as drama (Casablanca, In the Mood for Love, Love Letter). [aka melodrama of love]
A9-2 Romantic Comedy: love stories told for laughter (When Harry Met Sally..., Notting Hill). [aka romcom; chick flick]
### A10 Comedy — Axis: comic mode.
A10-1 Comedy Film: films aimed first at laughter outside the narrower leaves (Groundhog Day, Tampopo). [aka ]
A10-2 Comedy-Drama: films that balance comedy with serious drama (Tokyo Sonata). [aka dramedy]
A10-3 Black Comedy & Satire: comedy of death, taboo and social satire (Dr. Strangelove, The Funeral, The Taste of Tea). [aka dark comedy; satire]
A10-4 Screwball Comedy: the fast-talking romantic comedies of the 1930s–40s, American above all, and their European kin, battle of the sexes and comedy of remarriage (It Happened One Night, Bringing Up Baby, His Girl Friday). [aka comedy of remarriage]
A10-5 Slapstick Comedy: physical comedy of pratfalls, chases and sight gags, from the silent clowns to Tati and Mr. Bean (The General, City Lights, Duck Soup, Playtime). [aka silent comedy; physical comedy]
A10-6 Parody & Spoof: comedy that mocks a genre or a famous work (Airplane!, Young Frankenstein, Monty Python and the Holy Grail, Scary Movie). [aka spoof film; parody film]
A10-7 Teen & Sex Comedy: comedy of adolescence, parties and sexual initiation (Animal House, Fast Times at Ridgemont High, American Pie, Superbad). [aka teen comedy; sex comedy; gross-out comedy]
A10-8 Commedia all'italiana: Italian comedy of 1958–1980 that satirised postwar society — Monicelli, Risi, Germi, Scola (Big Deal on Madonna Street, Divorce Italian Style, Il Sorpasso). [aka Italian-style comedy]
A10-9 Action Comedy: comedy carried by action set pieces outside the buddy-cop cycle (Beverly Hills Cop, The Nice Guys, Kung Fu Hustle). [aka ]
A10-10 Crime Comedy: comedy of criminals, capers and botched crimes (The Ladykillers, A Fish Called Wanda, Snatch, The Big Lebowski). [aka caper comedy]

A10-13 Bourekas Film: the Israeli comedy and melodrama cycle of the 1960s–70s built on Mizrahi–Ashkenazi ethnic stereotypes (Sallah Shabati, Snooker, Charlie and a Half). [aka bourekas]
A10-12 Stand-Up Comedy Film: recorded stand-up comedy performances released as films or specials (Richard Pryor: Live in Concert, Eddie Murphy Raw, Nanette). [aka stand-up special; comedy special]
### A11 Musical & Music — Axis: how music carries the film.
A11-1 Musical & Music Film: films carried by song and music: stage and screen musicals and films about musicians and bands (Singin' in the Rain, La La Land, Linda Linda Linda). [aka musical film; music film]
A11-2 Dance Film: films built on dance and dancers, competitions and the dance floor (The Red Shoes, Saturday Night Fever, Dirty Dancing, Step Up). [aka dance movie]
A11-3 Rumberas Film: the Mexican cabaret melodrama of the 1940s–50s built on rumba dancers and the nightclub (Aventurera, Victims of Sin). [aka cabaretera film; cine de rumberas]
### A12 Erotic Film — Axis: the circuit it was made for.
A12-1 Pink Film: pinku eiga: independent Japanese softcore features made from the 1960s for the pink-film circuit, a training ground for directors (Daydream). [aka pinku eiga]
A12-2 Roman Porno: Nikkatsu's studio line of erotic features (1971–1988) under which Kumashiro, Tanaka and Sone made auteur films (Flower and Snake, A Woman Called Sada Abe). [aka Nikkatsu Roman Porno; romanporno]
A12-3 Erotic Drama: art-house and mainstream films whose subject is sexual obsession (In the Realm of the Senses, Last Tango in Paris, The Housemaid). [aka erotic film]
A12-4 Sexploitation: low-budget softcore features made for sex appeal outside Japan — nudie-cuties, Russ Meyer, Euro-softcore (Faster, Pussycat! Kill! Kill!, Emmanuelle, Vixen!). [aka softcore film; sexploitation film]
A12-5 Pornographic Film: hardcore features, chiefly of the Golden Age of Porn (1969–1984) that reached mainstream cinemas (Deep Throat, Behind the Green Door, The Devil in Miss Jones). [aka Golden Age of Porn; porno chic]
### A13 Documentary — Axis: mode and subject.
A13-1 Documentary Film: non-fiction films outside the narrower leaves: social, political, historical and portrait documentaries (Shoah, Hoop Dreams, The Act of Killing). [aka ]
A13-2 Concert & Music Documentary: documentaries of musicians, concerts and festivals (Woodstock, Stop Making Sense, The Last Waltz, Summer of Soul). [aka concert film; rockumentary]
A13-3 Nature Documentary: documentaries of wildlife and the natural world (Microcosmos, March of the Penguins, My Octopus Teacher). [aka wildlife documentary]
A13-4 Direct Cinema & Cinéma Vérité: observational documentaries of the 1960s–70s with light cameras and synchronous sound (Primary, Salesman, Grey Gardens, Chronicle of a Summer). [aka cinéma vérité; observational documentary; direct cinema]
A13-5 True Crime Documentary: documentaries investigating real crimes and miscarriages of justice (The Thin Blue Line, Paradise Lost, Capturing the Friedmans). [aka true crime]
A13-6 Essay Film: personal, reflective non-fiction that argues and wanders (Night and Fog, Sans Soleil, F for Fake, The Gleaners and I). [aka film essay]
A13-7 City Symphony: 1920s–30s documentaries that render a city's day as visual rhythm (Berlin: Symphony of a Metropolis, Man with a Movie Camera, Manhatta). [aka city symphony film]
A13-8 Mondo Film: Italian-born shockumentaries of bizarre customs and violence, 1962 onward (Mondo Cane, Africa Addio, Faces of Death). [aka shockumentary]
A13-9 Ethnographic Film: documentaries of peoples and cultures made from fieldwork (Nanook of the North, Moi, un noir, Dead Birds). [aka visual anthropology]
### A14 Drama — Axis: the drama's subject (family, youth, society, the individual).
A14-1 Shomingeki: shomin-geki: dramas of ordinary lower-middle-class urban life, centred on the family and the workplace, the home genre of Ozu, Naruse and Kinoshita (Tokyo Story, Floating Clouds). [aka shomin-geki; home drama]
A14-2 Family Drama: dramas of families, parents and children outside the classic shomingeki (Kramer vs. Kramer, Shoplifters, A Separation). [aka family film (drama)]
A14-3 Coming-of-Age Film: youth, school and teen films about growing up (The 400 Blows excepted as New Wave; Stand by Me, Lady Bird, Kids Return). [aka seishun eiga; youth film; teen film]
A14-4 Social Drama: dramas of social conditions, poverty, work, institutions and injustice (Ikiru, Parasite, I, Daniel Blake). [aka social problem film; social realism]
A14-5 Character Study: dramas centred on one person's inner life, grief, solitude or change (Taxi Driver, Drive My Car, Perfect Days). [aka psychological drama; art-house drama]
A14-6 Melodrama: emotionally heightened dramas of suffering and sacrifice, especially the women's film (Sirk, haha-mono mother films, Imitation of Life, The Life of Oharu). [aka haha-mono; women's film; weepie; chick flick (drama)]
A14-7 Sports Film: films built on a sport and its competition, comic or dramatic (Rocky, Raging Bull, Moneyball) (F19). [aka ]
A14-8 Animal Film: live-action films centred on an animal and its bond with people (The Story of Hachikō, The Adventures of Milo and Otis). [aka dog film; animal story]
A14-9 Taiyōzoku Film: the 'Sun Tribe' youth films of 1956 adapted from Ishihara Shintarō — affluent, amoral postwar youth (Season of the Sun, Crazed Fruit). [aka Sun Tribe films; taiyōzoku eiga]
A14-10 Legal Drama: dramas built on a trial, a lawyer's case or a jury (12 Angry Men, To Kill a Mockingbird, Anatomy of a Murder, Anatomy of a Fall) (F19). [aka courtroom drama; legal film]
A14-11 Road Movie: films whose structure is a journey on the road, comic or dramatic (Easy Rider excepted as New Hollywood; Thelma & Louise, Paris, Texas, Wild Strawberries, Y tu mamá también) (F19). [aka road film]
A14-14 Banlieue Film: French films of the multi-ethnic housing estates on the cities' edge, their youth, police and violence (La Haine, Girlhood, Divines, Les Misérables 2019). [aka cinéma de banlieue]
A14-12 Christian Film: films made by and for Christian audiences, of conversion and faith (The Passion of the Christ excepted as biblical epic; God's Not Dead, Fireproof). [aka faith-based film]
A14-13 Political Drama: dramas of politics and power, of leaders, campaigns and activists, outside the thriller (Mr. Smith Goes to Washington, The Candidate, Milk excepted as biopic, Battle of Algiers excepted as Third Cinema). [aka political film]
### A15 Animated Feature — Axis: technique and tradition (non-anime). Built at scale.
A15-1 Hand-Drawn Animated Feature: two-dimensional features animated by hand on cels or paper, or drawn digitally in 2D (Flash, Toon Boom, digital cut-out silhouettes), the Disney tradition and its rivals and the European, Soviet and Chinese studios (Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, The Lion King, The Snow Queen, The Triplets of Belleville). [aka traditional animation; cel animation]
A15-2 Computer-Animated Feature: features made with 3D computer animation, from Toy Story (1995) on: Pixar, DreamWorks, Disney and their peers (Shrek, Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse, Ne Zha). [aka CGI animation; 3D animation]
A15-3 Stop-Motion Feature: features animated frame by frame with puppets, clay or cut-outs (The Nightmare Before Christmas, Coraline, Chicken Run, Fantastic Mr. Fox). [aka stop-motion animation; claymation; puppet animation]
A15-4 Adult Animated Feature: animated features made for adults — satire, sex, politics, memoir — whatever the technique (Fritz the Cat, Persepolis, Anomalisa, Flee excepted as documentary). [aka adult animation]
### A16 Experimental & Avant-Garde — Axis: practice.
A16-1 Experimental Film: avant-garde and underground films that work outside narrative convention (Dog Star Man, Wavelength, Terayama's Emperor Tomato Ketchup). [aka avant-garde film; underground film]
A16-2 Surrealist Film: films of the surrealist movement and its heirs, dream logic and provocation (L'Âge d'Or, The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie, Eraserhead, The Holy Mountain). [aka surrealist cinema]
### A17 Western — Axis: setting (the American frontier and its transpositions).
A17-1 Western: the classical western of the American frontier: gunfighters, cavalry, cattle drives and settlers (Stagecoach, The Searchers, Rio Bravo). [aka cowboy film; horse opera]
A17-2 Spaghetti Western: Italian westerns of 1964–1978 — Leone, Corbucci, Sollima — with mercenary heroes, operatic violence and Morricone scores (A Fistful of Dollars, The Good, the Bad and the Ugly, Django, Once Upon a Time in the West). [aka Italian western; macaroni western]
A17-3 Revisionist Western: westerns that question the genre's myths — violence, race, the frontier (The Wild Bunch, McCabe & Mrs. Miller, Little Big Man, Unforgiven). [aka anti-western; acid western]
A17-4 Neo-Western: films set in the modern American West that carry the western's codes (Lonely Are the Brave, No Country for Old Men, Hell or High Water, Brokeback Mountain excepted as romance) (F20). [aka contemporary western; modern western]
A17-5 Ostern: Soviet and East German westerns and 'Eastern' adventures (White Sun of the Desert, The Sons of Great Bear). [aka Red western; Indianerfilm; Eastern]
### A18 Popular Cinema Modes — Axis: the named popular-cinema mode — a production culture the field names as a genre of its own (F13).
A18-1 Masala Film: South Asian popular cinema that mixes action, romance, comedy, melodrama and song-and-dance in one star vehicle — the Hindi, Telugu, Tamil and other Indian industries, and the same mode in Bangladesh (Dhallywood) and Pakistan (Lollywood) (Sholay, Amar Akbar Anthony, Baahubali, RRR, Pushpa) (F13). [aka masala movie; Bollywood masala; mass film; Bollywood film; Hindi film; song-and-dance film]
A18-2 Nollywood Film: Nigerian popular film made in the Nollywood mode: the home-video melodramas of family, wealth, the occult and the city from Living in Bondage (1992), and the 'New Nollywood' cinema releases that grew from them (The Wedding Party, King of Boys) (F13). [aka Nigerian video film; New Nollywood]
A18-3 Yeşilçam Film: Turkish popular cinema of the Istanbul studios, c. 1950–1990: star melodramas, comedies and adventure films made fast for a mass audience (Hababam Sınıfı, Selvi Boylum Al Yazmalım, Tosun Paşa). [aka Yeşilçam]
### A19 Children's & Family — Axis: occasion and audience (live action).
A19-1 Children's Film: live-action films made first for children (The Wizard of Oz excepted as fantasy; Mary Poppins excepted as musical; Matilda, Paddington, Kes excepted as drama). [aka family film; kids' film]
A19-2 Christmas Film: films built on Christmas, comic, romantic or dramatic (It's a Wonderful Life, Miracle on 34th Street, Home Alone, Elf) (F18). [aka holiday film]

## B Short Film — test: a film of 40 minutes or less (the Academy's line), fiction, animation, documentary or experimental; Japanese animated shorts go to E Anime.
  key: 1. Animated? → B1 Animated Short, by tradition and technique.
  key: 2. Non-fiction (actualities, documentary shorts)? → B3 Documentary & Actuality Short.
  key: 3. Avant-garde, outside narrative convention? → B4 Experimental Short.
  key: 4. Otherwise live-action fiction → B2 Live-Action Short.
### B1 Animated Short — Axis: tradition and technique.
B1-1 Theatrical Cartoon: studio cartoon shorts made for cinema programmes, the golden age of American animation and its kin (Disney's Silly Symphonies, Looney Tunes, Tom and Jerry, Fleischer's Betty Boop). [aka cartoon short; animated short subject]
B1-2 Auteur Animated Short: independent and art animation shorts by individual animators and national studios — NFB, Soyuzmultfilm, Zagreb School (Neighbours, Hedgehog in the Fog, The Man Who Planted Trees). [aka independent animation; art animation]
B1-3 Stop-Motion & Puppet Short: shorts animated with puppets, clay or objects (A Grand Day Out, The Wrong Trousers, Dimensions of Dialogue). [aka claymation short]
B1-4 Computer-Animated Short: shorts made with 3D computer animation (Luxo Jr., Geri's Game, Bao). [aka CGI short; Pixar short]
B1-5 Animated Television Special: animated specials made for television broadcast, chiefly holiday specials (A Charlie Brown Christmas, How the Grinch Stole Christmas!, Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer). [aka TV special (animated); holiday special]
### B2 Live-Action Short — Axis: mode (trick film, silent comedy, narrative short).
B2-1 Trick Film: early fantasy shorts built on camera tricks and stage illusion, Méliès and his rivals (A Trip to the Moon, The Haunted Castle). [aka féerie; trick film]
B2-2 Silent Comedy Short: one- and two-reel comedies of the silent clowns and the early sound comedy teams (One Week, The Immigrant, Laurel and Hardy's The Music Box). [aka slapstick short; comedy short]
B2-3 Narrative Short Film: live-action fiction shorts of every genre (La Jetée, The Red Balloon, Six Shooter). [aka short film; short subject]
### B3 Documentary & Actuality Short — Axis: mode.
B3-1 Actuality Film: the non-fiction views of early cinema, 1890s–1900s (Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory, The Arrival of a Train, Carmencita). [aka actualité; actuality]
B3-2 Documentary Short: documentaries under 40 minutes (Night and Fog, Glass, The Silent World excepted as feature). [aka short documentary]
### B4 Experimental Short — Axis: practice.
B4-1 Avant-Garde Short: experimental and avant-garde shorts — surrealist, abstract, structural and underground (Un Chien Andalou, Meshes of the Afternoon, Scorpio Rising, Window Water Baby Moving). [aka experimental short film]

PRECEDENCE
F1 Named cycle before movement: A film with a home in a named genre cycle (chanbara, kaidan, J-horror, yakuza, film noir, pink film, Roman Porno, pinky violence, kaiju, tokusatsu, Taiyōzoku) goes to that cycle, even when a movement's reference sources list it as a member. A movement leaf keeps the attested members with no cycle home; every attested member carries the movement as a tag (D1).
F2 Named cycle before general genre: A named cycle with its own canon (J-horror, kaiju eiga, yakuza eiga, pink film) beats the general genre leaf (supernatural horror, monster film, gangster film, sexploitation).
F3 Ghosts before period: A film whose story turns on ghosts or the supernatural is horror (kaidan eiga) even when set in the Edo period; period setting alone does not make jidaigeki win.
F4 Swords before period: A jidaigeki whose drama is carried by sword combat is chanbara; a period film of manners, politics or family is jidaigeki.
F5 Giant monsters: A film built on a giant monster (kaiju) is Kaiju Film whether or not a costumed hero appears; a costumed transforming hero with no giant monster at its centre is Tokusatsu Hero Film.
F6 Crime vs thriller: Criminals or police and their world at the centre → Crime; suspense built on a threat to an ordinary protagonist, a conspiracy or a puzzle → Thriller & Mystery.
F7 Drama vs romance vs comedy: A love story as the main line → Romance; aimed at laughter first → Comedy; otherwise Drama, divided by its subject.
F9 Framing devices: A fantastical or science-fiction device that only frames a love story or a youth drama (a time slip, a letter from the future) does not make the film fantasy or science fiction: it is placed by the story it frames (A9, A14-3).
F10 Leaf examples follow the key: When a film named as a leaf example would be sent elsewhere by the key, the key wins and the example is replaced; examples never override the key.
F8 Anime: Japanese animation is always E Anime, never an animated-feature leaf, whatever list it was selected from.
F11 Pre-modern wars in Japan: A war fought in Japan's pre-1868 past is jidaigeki or chanbara, not War Film (Ran, Kagemusha).
F12 Cycle before comedy: A genre cycle wins over Comedy when the cycle's premise drives the plot (Zom 100 → Zombie Film; Shaun of the Dead → Zombie Film). Comedy keeps films whose premise is only a backdrop for gags; a horror film played mainly for laughs with no cycle home is Comedy Horror.
F13 Popular-cinema modes: A South Asian popular star vehicle that mixes an action strand with romance, comedy, melodrama and song-and-dance is Masala Film, before the general crime, action, fantasy and historical leaves (Sholay, Baahubali, Pushpa). Without an action strand, a family or romance musical goes by its main line with Masala Film as see-also (Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge → Romantic Drama). A film built on one narrow premise keeps that leaf with Masala Film as see-also (heist, spy, superhero, sport, revenge: Dhoom, Pathaan, Krrish, Dangal). A Nigerian film made in the Nollywood mode is Nollywood Film unless a named genre cycle claims it. Films outside those modes (parallel cinema, art-house, realist thrillers) are placed by genre like any other film.
F14 Biopics: A film that tells a real person's life or career is Biographical Film whatever the person's field (musicians, athletes, criminals), unless it is mainly a war film, a sports contest or a named cycle; a dramatised single real event is placed by its genre (a heist → Heist Film; a battle → War Film).
F15 Superheroes: A film whose protagonist has a costumed or super-powered heroic identity is Superhero Film, before Action, Science Fiction and Fantasy; a superhero parody stays there too. Japanese transforming heroes are Tokusatsu (F5).
F16 Spies: Espionage as the plot's engine makes a Spy Film, before Action and Political Thriller (James Bond, Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy).
F17 Animation: Every animated feature that is not Japanese animation is Animated Feature (A15), placed by technique, whatever its genre; animated documentaries are Documentary (key step 1). Genre is a see-also.
F18 Children's and Christmas films: A live-action film made first for children (not a family-friendly fantasy or adventure, which keep their genre) is Children's Film; a film built on Christmas is Christmas Film, before Comedy, Romance and Drama.
F19 Road, sport and court: A film whose structure is a journey is Road Movie, a film built on a sport and its competition is Sports Film, and a film built on a trial is Legal Drama, whether comic or dramatic, unless a named cycle claims it.
F21 Genre films in wartime: A war film is about the war and the soldiers' experience of it; a horror, action or thriller film that merely uses a war as its setting goes by its own genre (Sisu → Action Film; Overlord → Science Fiction Horror).
F22 Narrow before broad: When a film's defining trait is named by a narrow leaf that the key reaches only later (Crime Comedy, Action Comedy, Parody & Spoof, Black Comedy & Satire, Sports Film, Dance Film, Spy Film, Road Movie), that leaf wins over the broader sub-family reached first: The Olsen Gang → Crime Comedy, a Karl May spoof → Parody & Spoof, a fencing biopic → Sports Film; a war satire (The Great Dictator, Jojo Rabbit) → Black Comedy & Satire; a spy comedy → Parody & Spoof when it mocks the genre (Johnny English, Spy), otherwise Action Comedy.
F23 Subgenres are not cycles: Subgenre leaves (vampire, zombie, slasher, haunted house, creature feature, found footage) are not named cycles for F1/D1: an attested movement member that is also a vampire film stays in the movement (Nosferatu → German Expressionism). Named cycles are the period- and industry-bound leaves (giallo, J-horror, poliziottesco, spaghetti western, Ostern, blaxploitation, erotic thriller ...); revisionist and neo-Western are subgenres; a later foreign remake or homage of a cycle film goes by its own genre, with the cycle as see-also (The Grudge 2019).
F24 Romance or costume: An original love story in a period setting is Romantic Drama (The English Patient, Portrait of a Lady on Fire); Costume Drama takes the drama of manners and society, above all literary adaptations (Austen, James, Wharton, Forster: A Room with a View, The Age of Innocence). A period comedy goes by its comic mode.
F25 Gimmick comedies: A comedy whose fantastical or speculative premise only carries the gags is Comedy (Groundhog Day, Multiplicity, Bedazzled, Ivan Vasilyevich Changes His Profession), with the genre as see-also; a science-fiction action comedy is Action Comedy (Men in Black). A live-action/animation hybrid is Animated Feature only when animation carries most of the film (Who Framed Roger Rabbit yes, Space Jam yes, Enchanted no).
F26 Action vehicles: A star vehicle carried by fights, chases and stunts is Action even when its hero is a cop or a criminal (Die Hard, Speed, Cobra); Crime takes films whose subject is the crime, its world or its investigation. Disaster films are A5-5 whether the catastrophe is speculative or real (Airport, The Towering Inferno, Titanic excepted as romance).
F27 Made-for-TV children's films and compilations: A children's television film (Disney Channel and Nickelodeon originals, Halloweentown) is Children's Film whatever its premise; theatrical family fantasy and science fiction keep their genre (F18). A feature assembled from television episodes is excluded as a compilation (the episodes belong to their series); an animated television special under 40 minutes is B1-5.
F20 Westerns: A film set on the American frontier (or a transposition of its codes to another frontier — the Australian bushranger film, Aferim!) is a Western leaf before Action, Historical Drama and Comedy; a contemporary crime film in the West is Neo-Western only when the frontier code carries it (Hell or High Water, No Country for Old Men).
AN1 Battle before world: A series whose episodes are built on escalating fights, rivals and power systems (shōnen battle) is E1 even when set in a fantasy world (Fullmetal Alchemist, Demon Slayer, Jujutsu Kaisen); an isekai or a fantasy whose world and journey carry it stays E3 (Re:Zero, Frieren).
AN2 Franchise unity: All seasons, films, OVAs and specials of one franchise share the leaf of its main series, unless a spin-off belongs to another genre outright (a gag spin-off → E10).
AN3 Demographic: Shōnen, seinen, shōjo and josei are tags on every title (MAL/AniList demographic); they divide leaves only where the field names a demographic genre (shōjo romance, josei).
AN5 Comedy first: A series whose premise is a pretext for gags or parody (gag manga, a genre parody) is E10 whatever its setting or club (Detroit Metal City, Dragon Half, KonoSuba excepted as isekai, since its world carries the story).
AN6 Children's franchises: A children's franchise (Pokémon, Digimon, Doraemon) stays in E11 across its entries (AN2), including films made for its grown-up fans.
AN7 What carries the story: A setting, sport, band or speculative frame that is only a backdrop does not decide the leaf: the sport must drive the plot for E7 (Blue Box → romance), a fantasy world must carry the story for E3 (a villainess romance in a fantasy court → romance). Toy-line battle shows for children (Beyblade, Bakugan) are E11; one-off comedy specials and chibi extras of a franchise are E10.
AN8 Adult flag: E12 holds explicit hentai only; an adult-flagged art film or erotic drama (Cleopatra 1970, Shoujo Tsubaki) is placed by its genre with E12-1 as see-also. A gag series about everyday life is E10 (comedy first, AN5); a gentle everyday series without gag structure is E6.
AN9 Battle structure in any setting: AN1 holds for science-fiction and samurai settings too: a series built on escalating fights and power systems is E1 (Kaiju No. 8, Jigokuraku); a death game or gambling series whose engine is the game, not the fights, is E8-6 (Kakegurui, Juuni Taisen excepted as battle). Ecchi series whose fan service carries them stay E10-2 even with fights (High School DxD).
AN10 Frames and parodies: A magical-girl or robot frame on a romance or comedy does not make E4 or E2 (AN7); a magical-girl parody is E10. A historical war epic whose era and campaigns are the subject is E8-4 even with constant battles (Kingdom, Vinland Saga). Comedy shorts, bloopers and chibi extras of a franchise are E10; side stories in the franchise's own genre stay with it (AN2).
AN4 Non-Japanese animation in the anime database: Donghua and Korean animation selected through the anime database are placed by the same key, with country as a filter; animation from elsewhere selected as film is A15.
TV1 Animation: Every animated series that is not anime is C6, whatever its genre (The Simpsons → Animated Sitcom; Arcane → Animated Action-Adventure).
TV2 Serial or series: A long-running serial in a named serial tradition (telenovela, dizi, daily soap, soap opera, teleserye, musalsal) is D, not C, even when it is also a crime or historical story; a limited or seasonal series with a closed arc is C.
TV3 Named Asian tradition before genre: An Asian drama in a named tradition of C2 stays there whatever its genre mix (a sageuk with zombies, Kingdom, is Sageuk; a xianxia romance is Wuxia & Xianxia Drama); a Korean thriller or a Japanese medical drama with no tradition home goes by genre.
TV4 Crime before drama: A series centred on crime, police, lawyers defending criminals or spies is C3, even when its prestige makes it 'drama' (The Sopranos, The Wire, Breaking Bad); a legal series about the law firm's own life is Legal Drama.
TV6 Daily comedies, crime comedies and true events: A daily or long-running comedy is a sitcom (C4), not a serial tradition, even with thousands of episodes (Taarak Mehta Ka Ooltah Chashmah). A comedy about criminals goes by laughter first (C4). A dramatised real crime is True Crime Drama (C3-7); a dramatised real event that is not a crime is Docudrama Series (C1-9).
TV7 Non-fiction: A documentary series is C8; a talk, chat or variety programme that is not a cross-border format goes to G3 (variety and talk) as a programme of its own.
TV8 What carries the story: A comedy whose fantastical premise only carries the gags is Comedy (C4), not Genre Series (The Addams Family, Wonderfalls); a supernatural or speculative frame on a medical, crime or family series does not make it C5 unless the frame carries the plot. A children's cartoon with an adult following stays Children's Animated Series. News magazines and current-affairs programmes are not series of this tab: exclude them.
TV9 Comedy first; thrillers; long-running continuing dramas: A sitcom with a science-fiction, fantasy or horror premise is Comedy (C4) when laughter comes first (Red Dwarf, The League of Gentlemen); comic detectives stay in C3 with C4-5 as see-also. A domestic or psychological thriller miniseries is C3-8. A long-running continuing drama of a workplace (Casualty, Holby City, The Bill) goes by genre (C1-2, C3-1), not D. An Arab 30-episode Ramadan series is D5-1 whatever its genre.
TV5 Franchise unity: Spin-offs and revivals share their parent's leaf unless they change genre outright.
TV10 Ramadan comedies, dizi genres, Arab serials: TV6 wins over TV9 for Ramadan comedies: a Ramadan sitcom or sketch show is C4; only Ramadan drama is D5-1. A Turkish weekly serial in the dizi format stays D3 whatever its genre (crime, action, romantic comedy); only a case-of-the-week police show is C3-1. D3-2 is any dizi set before 1923.
TV11 Biography, youth and remakes: A biographical or religious series of a pre-modern life (prophets, saints, Leonardo) is Period Drama (C1-6); a modern life is Docudrama (C1-9). Series about young adults past school age (college, aspirants, first jobs) go by genre (C1-12, C4-5), not Teen Drama. A remake of a series shares its original's leaf unless it changes genre (TV5).
TV12 Streaming serials, Christmas calendars: A streaming serial of 60 or more episodes in a named serial tradition is D; a short web series of the same subject is C. A Nordic Advent serial is C7-3 whatever its genre. A short mythological web series is C5-2 with D4-2 as see-also.
F28 Stage recordings, specials and visual albums: A filmed stage production goes by its stage genre (a musical → A11-1, a stand-up show → A10-12, a play → its genre). A special of a television series that is not a compilation (the South Park streaming specials) is placed with its series → route:FT-C; a music or variety television special is excluded as television. A visual album or long-form music video is Musical Film (A11-1) as a feature, Live-Action Short (B2-3) at 40 minutes or less.
F29 Creatures, homages and revenge: Dinosaurs, giant sharks and other natural monsters are Creature Feature (A4-14) when the film aims to frighten, Science Fiction (A5-3) when it is adventure; only giant monsters of the kaiju tradition are A5-1. A recent homage to a cycle (Malignant, MaXXXine) goes by its own genre with the cycle as see-also (F23). Revenge horror stays horror (A4) when it aims to frighten; a revenge story without horror is A3-5.
TV13 Unscripted programmes and edge genres: A talk, variety, reality, court, instructional or web show is placed in G directly, whether or not it is a licensed format (G2-2 for occupational and paranormal reality, G3-4 for court shows, G3-5 for how-to shows); professional wrestling is G3-1. A documentary series with reenactments is C8 when talking heads and archive carry it, C1-9 when dramatised scenes do. A disaster miniseries is Action & Adventure (C1-15). A US re-edit or dub of an anime is anime (route:FT-E). A defence-lawyer series is Legal Drama (C1-3) when the cases change each week, Crime Drama (C3-3) when a season follows one crime; a lawyer dramedy stays C1-3 with C4-5 as see-also. A 2000s–2010s network serial of secrets among the rich (Revenge, Gossip Girl) is Prime-Time Soap Opera (D2-4); a teen mystery (Pretty Little Liars) is Thriller (C3-6).
TV14 Streaming originals in a serial tradition; sports: A streaming original of a closed season (8–20 episodes) from a serial-tradition country (a Netflix or BluTV Turkish series, a streaming telenovela) goes by genre in C; the broadcast weekly or daily serial is D. A remake that leaves its original's tradition goes by its own form (a 60-episode Turkish broadcast remake of Doctor Foster is D3-1). An anthology of crime or suspense stories is C1-16 Anthology with the genre as see-also. Live sports coverage is not a series of this tab: exclude it.
TV15 C-drama traditions: A Chinese drama of the Party, the revolution or national achievement is Main-Melody Drama (C2-12) whatever its genre; one set in 1912–1949 is Republican-Era Drama (C2-13); a modern Chinese romance of any kind is C2-6. A censored danmei adaptation goes by its setting (The Untamed → C2-5) with C2-11 as see-also; a modern-day one is C2-11. A costume drama goes to C2-5 when martial heroes or a fantasy world carry the story, otherwise C2-4.
TV16 Remakes, spin-offs, rich-family serials: A foreign remake keeps its original's leaf only when it keeps its form; it goes by its own setting when moved (a US remake of a Nordic series is C3-1, not C3-4). A spin-off that moves to another workplace goes by its own profession. A streaming or 2020s series of rich-family secrets with a closed season is C (C1-1 or C3-8) with D2-4 as see-also; the D2-4 rule of TV13 holds only for network serials of 2000–2019 and long-running streaming serials (TV12).
TV17 Japanese and Hong Kong drama; tokusatsu: Every Japanese drama of romance, school, work, family or everyday life is C2-9; Japanese crime, mystery, medical, legal and speculative dramas go by genre with C2-9 as see-also. A Hong Kong drama is C2-14 unless it is wuxia (C2-5); Hong Kong BL is C2-11. A tokusatsu series is C5-6 whatever its audience (Power Rangers included); a tokusatsu parody comedy is C4. A foreign remake of an Asian drama made inside another tradition goes to the remaking tradition (a Japanese remake of a K-drama is C2-9) with the original's leaf as see-also. A New Year or one-off special is route:FT-A; a re-edit or complete edition of a series is excluded as a duplicate.
TV18 Korean drama boundaries: A Korean weekend or daily family drama is Makjang (C2-3) only when birth secrets, revenge or chaebol schemes drive it; a gentle family drama is C1-1 with C2-3 as see-also. Sageuk (C2-2) ends with the Korean Empire (1910); a drama of the colonial period or later is Period Drama (C1-6) with C2-2 as see-also. A Korean drama goes by genre (C3, C5) only when its genre plot carries it and the love line is secondary; otherwise it is K-Drama (C2-1). A Korean prosecutor or judge drama is Legal Drama (C1-3) unless it follows one crime (C3-3). A one- or two-part drama special is route:FT-A.
TV19 Thai and Philippine drama; versions and segments: A Thai drama of romance, youth or revenge is Lakorn (C2-10); a Thai thriller, horror or fantasy series that its genre carries goes by genre with C2-10 as see-also. Any Asian BL or GL series is C2-11 whatever its genre or form; a straight main couple with BL side couples stays in the tradition (C2-10) with C2-11 as see-also. A Philippine broadcast serial is D6-1; a Philippine streaming series goes by genre. Uncut, director's-cut and special-edition versions, anthology segments listed as titles, and bonus or making-of specials are excluded as duplicates or supplements of their series.
F30 Comic police and period settings: A comedy about police that is neither buddy-cop (A8-7) nor spoof is Comedy (A10-1), or Action Comedy (A10-9) when action set-pieces carry it. A past setting 'defines' a film (key step 8) only when the era's events or manners are its subject; a family drama or comedy merely set in the past goes by its genre with A7-4 as see-also (F24).
TV20 Unscripted edges: A hidden-camera, prank or stunt show is G2-4. A transformation story (house, body, business) is G2-3; a show that teaches the craft is G3-5. Recaps, re-edits with pop-up facts and behind-the-scenes companions of a format are excluded as supplements (TV19). A model or talent search judged on performance is G1-1; one built on living together and elimination tasks is G1-2.
